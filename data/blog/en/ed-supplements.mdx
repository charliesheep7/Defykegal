Walk into any pharmacy or scroll any men's health site and you will find a wall of ED supplements promising harder erections, more stamina, and "Viagra-level results, naturally." Most of it is marketing. But not all of it — a small handful of ingredients actually have research behind them.

The problem is telling the two apart. This is the honest, science-forward breakdown: which ED supplements have real evidence, which ones are a waste of money or genuinely risky, and where a capsule stops being the answer.

## What ED Supplements Actually Are

"ED supplement" is a loose label covering three very different things:

- **Single-ingredient amino acids and herbs** with some clinical research — L-arginine, L-citrulline, and Panax ginseng being the main ones.
- **Multi-herb "male enhancement" blends** sold in flashy packaging, usually with proprietary formulas and little to no evidence.
- **Products that are supplements in name only** — capsules secretly spiked with real prescription drugs.

That last category matters more than most men realize. Supplements are not held to the same approval standards as prescription or over-the-counter medications, so there is often no reliable way to know whether a given product is effective or even safe — and the amount of active ingredient can vary wildly between brands. "Natural" on the label guarantees nothing. The [NHS advises](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) seeing a doctor about persistent erection problems rather than self-treating, precisely because ED can signal an underlying condition worth checking.

The supplements worth discussing at all mostly share one mechanism: they aim to boost **nitric oxide**, the signaling molecule that relaxes the blood vessels in the penis so blood can flow in. That is the same pathway ED medications work on — which is exactly why the effective supplements are modest, and the fake ones cheat by adding the real drug.

## The Ones With Actual Evidence

A few ingredients have cleared the bar of randomized, placebo-controlled trials.

**L-arginine.** This amino acid is a direct precursor to nitric oxide. A [multicentre, double-blind, randomized controlled trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8995264/) gave men with vasculogenic ED high-dose L-arginine for three months and found significantly improved erectile function scores compared with placebo, in both moderate and severe cases. Reviews put the useful range at roughly 1,500 to 5,000 mg per day.

**L-citrulline.** Your body converts citrulline into arginine, and some men absorb it more efficiently, so it raises nitric oxide by a similar route. The evidence base is smaller than for arginine, but the mechanism is the same and small studies are encouraging.

**Panax (red) ginseng.** This is the single best-supported herb. A [systematic review of herbal remedies for ED](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8451697/) found Panax ginseng consistently improved erectile function scores versus placebo across multiple trials. It appears to work through nitric oxide and possibly hormonal pathways.

Even for these, be realistic: the effect is a modest edge, not a cure, and it does nothing for the mechanical side of an erection. If your ED is rooted in weak muscles or blood that leaks back out, a nitric-oxide bump only goes so far. ## The Ones to Skip — and the Real Risk

Some popular ED supplements are backed by side effects, not results.

- **Yohimbe / yohimbine.** [Healthline notes](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/yohimbe) it can cause side effects including high blood pressure, anxiety, and a rapid or irregular heartbeat, and it is not recommended without medical supervision. The risk often outweighs any modest benefit.
- **DHEA.** A hormone precursor with mixed evidence and real downsides — acne, hair loss, and suppression of your body's own hormone production, with unknown long-term safety.
- **Proprietary "male enhancement" blends.** If the label hides doses behind a "proprietary formula," you cannot judge the science, and you become the test subject.

Then there is the genuinely dangerous part. The [FDA maintains an ongoing list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/sexual-enhancement-and-energy-product-notifications) of sexual enhancement products found to contain hidden drug ingredients. One analysis identified undeclared pharmaceuticals in hundreds of supplements over a decade, with **sildenafil** — the drug in Viagra — the most common adulterant. That means a product marketed as an all-natural supplement can contain an unlabeled, unmeasured dose of a prescription drug.

Why that is not a lucky shortcut: combined with nitrate heart medications, hidden sildenafil can drop your blood pressure to dangerous levels. You have no idea how much you are taking, and neither does the store selling it. Any "supplement" that works as fast and hard as a prescription is a warning sign, not a bargain.

## What Actually Moves the Needle

Here is the part the supplement aisle will never tell you. The muscles at the base of the penis — part of your [pelvic floor](/blog/pelvic-floor-muscles) — physically clamp down to trap blood in the shaft and keep an erection firm. When they are weak, blood flows in and then leaks back out, and no amount of nitric oxide fixes a drainage problem.

This is why pelvic floor training has some of the strongest evidence of any natural approach to ED. The landmark study by **Dorey and colleagues (2004), published in BJU International**, found that pelvic floor exercises restored normal erectile function in **40% of men** with ED and significantly improved it in another **35.5%**. That is a bigger, more durable effect than any over-the-counter supplement can claim — and it treats a cause rather than renting a few hours of better blood flow.

If you do want to try an evidence-backed supplement like L-arginine or ginseng, treat it as a possible add-on, not the plan. The plan is training the muscles that hold an erection in place. Pair that with the basics that support blood flow everywhere — the same habits in our guide to [increasing libido in men](/blog/how-to-increase-libido-in-men) — and you are working on the actual machinery.

The catch with training is consistency. A pelvic floor program only works if you actually do it most days, and that is where most men fall off.

## What Progress Looks Like

Set expectations by the evidence, not the ad copy. Supplement studies for L-arginine and ginseng generally run **4 to 12 weeks** before measuring a difference — so instant results almost always mean a hidden drug.

Pelvic floor training follows a similar honest curve. Most men doing consistent daily sessions start noticing firmer erections and better control within **4 to 6 weeks**, with continued gains over a few months as the muscles strengthen. Nothing here is overnight, but unlike a capsule, the improvement compounds and does not wear off between doses. ## Common Questions

**Can I just take L-arginine instead of doing exercises?**
You can try it, and it may give a modest edge, but it does not address weak pelvic floor muscles — a common physical driver of soft erections. The strongest results come from training the muscles that trap blood, with a supplement as an optional extra rather than the whole strategy.

**Are "over the counter Viagra" supplements the real thing?**
No. Real sildenafil is prescription-only. Anything sold over the counter promising the same effect is either weak or has been spiked with an undeclared drug — which the FDA repeatedly finds and recalls. It is a safety gamble, not a legal shortcut.

**Is it safe to combine supplements with ED medication?**
Not without medical advice. Stacking nitric-oxide supplements or hidden-drug products on top of prescription ED medication can push blood pressure too low. If you are already on treatment, talk to a doctor before adding anything.

**Do any supplements increase penis size?**
No. No pill, capsule, or powder enlarges the penis — that claim is a reliable marker of a scam product. What you can improve is erection firmness and fullness, which comes from blood flow and pelvic floor strength, not supplements.

## The Bottom Line

A couple of ED supplements — L-arginine and Panax ginseng — have earned modest, real evidence. Most of the aisle has not, and a worrying slice of it is spiked with hidden prescription drugs. If you want to experiment with a well-studied ingredient, do it with eyes open and keep expectations small.

But the approach with the best evidence for lasting change is not something you swallow. It is training the pelvic floor muscles that actually hold an erection in place — the one variable a supplement can never touch.
