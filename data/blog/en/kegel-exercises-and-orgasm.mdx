Kegel exercises and orgasm are connected through muscle. The same pelvic floor muscles you train in a kegel contract rhythmically when you ejaculate, so a stronger, better-coordinated pelvic floor can mean more control over when and how you climax. The research is strongest on control and timing, thinner on raw intensity.

## What Happens in the Pelvic Floor During Orgasm

Orgasm and ejaculation are related but separate. A review in _Fertility and Sterility_ by [Alwaal, Breyer and Lue (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26385403/) describes orgasm as a peak pleasure sensation, and ejaculation as a two-phase process of emission and expulsion under complex neurological control. The expulsion phase involves rhythmic contractions of the pelvic floor muscles.

Those are the muscles you can train. They also support erections by compressing the base of the penis. So a better-trained floor affects both what happens before climax and the mechanics of the climax itself.

## What the Evidence Shows

### Ejaculatory Control

A systematic review in _Physiotherapy_ by [Myers and Smith (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30979506/) examined ten trials of pelvic floor muscle training in men with ED or premature ejaculation. The authors reported that all trials showed improvement in ED, and for premature ejaculation, most trials showed improvement, with cure rates varying widely between studies. Training protocols differed a lot, which limits how firmly you can compare them.

More recently, an eight-week study in _Sexual Medicine_ by [Lyu and colleagues (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41090039/) enrolled 199 men, including 149 with premature ejaculation. In acquired PE, median latency rose from 120 to 180 seconds. In lifelong PE it rose from 30 to 60 seconds. These are real gains, but notice that the lifelong group still finished at a minute, so training is not a magic switch.

### The Honest Limit

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis in _The Journal of Sexual Medicine_ (de Oliveira et al.) pooled five randomised trials and reported that pelvic floor training had a smaller effect on latency than drug-based control interventions. Medication may do more for some men, and the two are not mutually exclusive. See [premature ejaculation medication](/blog/pe-medication) for the other side of the picture.

### Intensity

Here the evidence is thin. No large trial has shown that kegels measurably amplify orgasm in healthy men. What exists is clinical logic (better muscle tone and coordination) and plenty of anecdotal reports. We would rather say that plainly than overpromise.

### After Prostate Surgery

One clear example of the link: orgasm-associated urine leakage, called climacturia, is a common side effect after radical prostatectomy. A [review in the Asian Journal of Andrology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32167075/) (Kannady and Clavell-Hernández, 2020) lists pelvic floor muscle training among the techniques that subjectively help. If you are in this situation, talk to your urologist first.

## How to Train for Control

1. **Find the muscle.** Stop the flow of urine midstream once to identify it, then do not practice that way regularly. Our [male kegel exercises guide](/blog/male-kegel-exercises) walks through it.
2. **Start with holds.** Squeeze for three seconds, relax fully for three seconds, ten reps, up to three sets a day.
3. **Add quick flicks.** Rapid one-second contractions train the reflexive response.
4. **Prioritise the release.** Let the muscle drop fully after each rep. Many men with timing problems carry too much tension, so read [how to relax your pelvic floor muscles](/blog/how-to-relax-pelvic-floor-muscles).
5. **Take it into partnered sex.** Once automatic, use gentle contraction and deliberate release to manage arousal alongside techniques like the [stop-start method](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation).

For a structured programme aimed at timing, see [kegel exercises for premature ejaculation](/blog/kegel-exercises-for-premature-ejaculation).

## What Progress Looks Like

In the studies above, benefits appeared over roughly eight weeks and beyond. Early signs are usually better awareness of the muscle and a more noticeable "point of no return." Real changes in timing come later. Keep a short note of how long sex lasts and how in control you feel each month.

If you notice pain, burning, or worsening urgency, stop and see a doctor, as a tight pelvic floor may need relaxation work instead.
