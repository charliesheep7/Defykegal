Most advice on how to stop premature ejaculation falls into two useless camps: vague distraction tricks ("think about baseball") or expensive quick fixes that wear off the moment you stop using them. Neither builds anything. The reflex that ends things early is still exactly as twitchy as it was before.

Here is the part that actually matters: ejaculation is a reflex, and reflexes involve muscles and timing you can train. The men who genuinely gain control do it by strengthening the pelvic floor and rehearsing control, not by chasing gimmicks. This guide walks through the methods with the best evidence behind them, and how to combine them.

## Why Premature Ejaculation Is Trainable

Ejaculation is not purely mental — it is a physical reflex, and your pelvic floor muscles are right in the middle of it. The same muscles that stop your urine stream and pulse during orgasm are the ones that fire during ejaculation. Weak or poorly controlled, they give you little ability to hold back. Strong and responsive, they become a brake you can actually press.

The research on this is genuinely strong. In a landmark study by Pastore and colleagues published in Therapeutic Advances in Urology, men with lifelong premature ejaculation completed a 12-week pelvic floor muscle rehabilitation program. The result: [82.5% regained control of their ejaculatory reflex](https://uroweb.org/news/lifelong-premature-ejaculation-can-be-treated-by-pelvic-floor-exercises), raising their average time from under 60 seconds to roughly 146 seconds — with no drugs involved. That is control built through training the muscle, and it is the foundation everything else sits on.

Behavioral techniques add a second layer. As [InformedHealth.org's review of premature ejaculation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547551/) notes, methods like the stop-start and squeeze techniques help many men by teaching them to recognize and manage arousal before the point of no return.

## The Step-by-Step Guide

Here is how to put the two proven levers — pelvic floor strength and arousal control — to work.

1. **Find the right muscles.** Next time you urinate, stop the flow midstream. The muscles you just used are your pelvic floor. Alternatively, tighten the muscles you would use to stop yourself from passing gas. That upward, inward squeeze — without clenching your abs, glutes, or thighs — is the correct movement. Our guide to [kegels for men](/blog/kegels-for-men) covers isolating them in detail.
2. **Build baseline strength.** Do slow contractions: squeeze, hold for 3-5 seconds, then fully relax for the same time. The release matters as much as the squeeze. Start with 8-10 reps, a few times a day.
3. **Add quick "flicks."** Once slow holds feel easy, train fast contractions — sharp squeeze, immediate release, 10 in a row. These build the rapid response you need to clamp down at the critical moment.
4. **Practice the stop-start technique.** Alone at first, stimulate yourself until you are close to ejaculating, then stop entirely until the urge subsides. Repeat three or four times before finishing. This trains you to feel the point of no return coming and back off in time — our deep dive on the [stop-start method for premature ejaculation](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation) covers the full protocol.
5. **Deploy your pelvic floor during sex.** As arousal climbs, use a firm pelvic floor contraction to help delay the reflex — the strength you built in training becomes a usable brake in the moment. Combine it with slowing your pace and breathing.
6. **Slow your breathing.** Fast, shallow breathing spikes arousal. Long, slow exhales calm the nervous system and buy you time. It sounds minor; it is not.

## Building the Habit

Here is the honest truth that separates results from frustration: **none of this works as a one-off.** Pelvic floor strength follows the same rule as any muscle — consistent, progressive training beats occasional intense effort. The men in the Pastore study trained for 12 weeks. That timeline is the point, not a footnote.

The problem is that pelvic floor training is invisible and easy to forget. There is no mirror feedback, no soreness, no obvious sign you did it. That is exactly why most men quit before the muscle ever gets strong.

## Common Mistakes

A few errors quietly sabotage most men's efforts:

- **Squeezing the wrong muscles.** If your abs, glutes, or thighs tense, you are not training the pelvic floor. Isolate it.
- **Skipping the release.** Holding constant tension without fully relaxing builds an overactive, tired floor — not a strong, responsive one. Relax completely between reps.
- **Only training strength, never control.** Raw strength without the stop-start practice leaves you strong but still unable to read your own arousal. You need both.
- **Quitting at week two.** The muscle needs weeks to change. Early on, little seems to happen — right before it starts working. Push through.

## Common Questions

**Is premature ejaculation permanent?** For most men, no. It is one of the most common and most treatable male sexual concerns, and training-based approaches help the majority of men who stick with them.

**Can anxiety cause it?** Yes, especially in acquired premature ejaculation. Performance anxiety speeds the reflex, and slower breathing plus the confidence that comes from real training both help break that loop.

**Should I use numbing sprays?** Topical agents can reduce sensation and buy time, and a doctor can advise on them. But they mask the reflex rather than retrain it — best used as a bridge while you build lasting control, not as the whole plan.

## The Bottom Line

Stopping premature ejaculation is not about willpower or distraction — it is about training a reflex. Strengthen the pelvic floor, rehearse arousal control with the stop-start technique, and give it the weeks the research says it takes. Do that consistently and the change sticks, because you built it into the muscle rather than borrowing it from a spray.
