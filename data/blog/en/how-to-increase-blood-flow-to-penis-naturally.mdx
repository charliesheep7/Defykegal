Nearly every article on increasing blood flow to the penis covers the same half of the problem: how to get more blood in. Eat these foods, do this cardio, take this supplement.

That advice is not wrong, but it misses the other half. An erection is a pressure system, and pressure depends on inflow *and* outflow. If blood drains back out as fast as it arrives, you can have flawless circulation and still lose firmness within minutes. Fixing that requires training a muscle group most men have never deliberately contracted.

Here is how to work both sides.

## Why This Works: The Two-Valve System

Urologists describe erectile function as a hydraulics problem — the standard framing in the urology literature is [maximizing inflow while minimizing outflow](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24521101/).

**Inflow** is chemical. Sexual stimulation triggers the release of nitric oxide from penile nerve endings and from the endothelium, the thin lining of your blood vessels. Nitric oxide relaxes the smooth muscle inside the corpus cavernosum, the arteries dilate, and blood floods in. Research on [nitric oxide and erectile function](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4806684/) identifies it as the principal factor driving blood into the penis. Anything that damages the endothelium — smoking, high blood sugar, high blood pressure, chronic inflammation — cuts nitric oxide availability and weakens the whole cascade.

**Outflow** is mechanical. As the chambers fill, they compress veins against the tough outer sheath of the penis. Two pelvic floor muscles — the ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus — clamp down on the base and finish the seal. Strong muscles hold pressure. Weak ones let it bleed off.

This is why men with clean cardiovascular workups can still lose erections partway through. The blood is arriving fine; it simply is not staying.

## Step-by-Step: Improving Both Sides

### Getting more blood in

1. **Walk 30 minutes a day, most days.** [Harvard Health reports](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/5-natural-ways-to-overcome-erectile-dysfunction) this is associated with a 41% drop in ED risk. Aerobic exercise improves endothelial function directly, which raises nitric oxide bioactivity — the exact mechanism your erections run on.

2. **Eat for your arteries, not for your libido.** Harvard's review of [nondrug approaches to improving erectile function](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/nondrug-approaches-to-improve-erectile-function) points to aerobic exercise and a plant-based diet as the substantiated options. Dietary nitrates in leafy greens and beetroot convert to nitric oxide in the body, which is the one nutritional lever with a real mechanism behind it.

3. **Get your numbers measured.** Blood pressure, fasting glucose, and cholesterol all damage the arteries feeding the penis silently, over years. Our piece on [high blood pressure and ED](/blog/does-high-blood-pressure-cause-ed) explains why the penile arteries show damage before the coronary arteries do.

4. **Stop smoking.** Nicotine constricts vessels and suppresses nitric oxide production. No amount of training compensates for it.

5. **Sleep seven hours.** Nocturnal erections during REM sleep oxygenate penile tissue and help maintain its elasticity. Skipping sleep skips that maintenance cycle.

### Keeping the blood there

6. **Find the right muscle.** The pelvic floor is the muscle group you would use to stop urine mid-stream or to lift your testicles slightly without moving your abdomen, glutes, or thighs. If your stomach tightens or your legs move, you have recruited the wrong muscles. Our guide to [kegels for men](/blog/kegels-for-men) walks through locating it correctly.

7. **Train short and long contractions.** Short pulses build fast-twitch response — the reflexive clamp that matters during sex. Longer 5 to 10 second holds build endurance for sustaining an erection. Both are needed; most men only ever do one.

8. **Train twice daily for three months.** This is the protocol from the randomised controlled trial by Dorey and colleagues in the [British Journal of General Practice (2004)](https://bjgp.org/content/54/508/819), where three months of twice-daily pelvic floor exercise with biofeedback significantly outperformed lifestyle advice alone. Follow-up reporting on that work found normal erectile function restored in about 40% of men and improved in a further 35%.

9. **Progress the load.** Muscles adapt to what they are used to. If you have done the same 10 reps for two months, you have stopped getting stronger. Increase hold time, rep count, or add contractions in harder positions — standing is harder than lying down.

## Building the Habit

The training works. The problem is that almost nobody keeps it up, because kegels are invisible, unmeasurable by feel, and easy to forget when nothing hurts.

Three things fix that:

- **Anchor it to something you already do.** Immediately after brushing your teeth, morning and night, matches the twice-daily protocol without needing willpower.
- **Track the reps.** If you are not counting, you are not progressing — you are just occasionally squeezing.
- **Commit to twelve weeks before evaluating.** The evidence base measures at three months. Judging at week two tells you nothing.

## Common Mistakes

**Chasing supplements instead of circulation.** The male enhancement category is largely unregulated and frequently adulterated with undeclared drugs. Our review of [vitamins for erectile strength](/blog/best-vitamins-for-erectile-strength) separates the modest real evidence from the marketing.

**Squeezing everything at once.** Clenching glutes, abs, and thighs alongside the pelvic floor is the single most common form error. It feels like effort while training almost nothing useful. Our [pelvic floor exercises for men](/blog/exercises-for-male-pelvic-floor) guide covers isolation.

**Training a floor that is already too tight.** If your pelvic floor is overactive rather than weak, more contraction worsens symptoms. Pain, urgency, and a semi-rigid penis at rest point that direction — check our guide on [telling if your pelvic floor is tight or weak](/blog/how-to-tell-if-pelvic-floor-is-tight-or-weak) first.

**Ignoring the vascular side entirely.** Pelvic floor training improves retention. It does not fix arteries narrowed by twenty years of high blood sugar. Both halves, or the results stall.

## Common Questions

**Does holding my breath during kegels help?** No — it recruits the wrong muscles and raises intra-abdominal pressure. Breathe normally throughout; exhale on the contraction if it helps you stay relaxed elsewhere.

**Is morning wood a good indicator of blood flow?** It is a useful one. Regular nocturnal erections suggest the vascular and nerve systems are working, which points toward a psychological or pelvic floor issue rather than an arterial one. Their disappearance is worth mentioning to a doctor.

**How does this compare to medication?** PDE5 inhibitors work on the inflow side by amplifying the nitric oxide signal. They do nothing for retention. The two approaches address different failure points, which is why some men respond partially to medication and fully once they add pelvic floor training. Increasing blood flow to the penis naturally means protecting the arteries that deliver it and strengthening the muscles that hold it. Walk daily, eat for your endothelium, get your numbers checked, and train the pelvic floor twice a day for three months. That combination has the best evidence behind it — and it is free.
