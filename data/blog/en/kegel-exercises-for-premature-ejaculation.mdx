Most men who try kegel exercises for premature ejaculation do them wrong in one specific way: they only train the squeeze.

Ejaculation is a reflex driven by rhythmic contractions of your pelvic floor. Control does not come from a stronger clench alone. It comes from **awareness** of that muscle, the ability to **relax** it during arousal, and the strength to **contract** it deliberately when you are close.

That combination is trainable. Clinical programs built around it have shown some of the best non-drug results for premature ejaculation. Here is how it works and how to do it.

## Why pelvic floor training works for PE

When you ejaculate, the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles at the base of the penis contract rhythmically to push semen out. These are pelvic floor muscles — the same ones you train with kegels.

Men with lifelong premature ejaculation often have poor voluntary control over this area. Training it does two things: it improves your ability to sense rising tension before the reflex fires, and it gives you a muscle you can consciously engage or release.

The evidence comes largely from an Italian urology team led by Antonio Pastore:

- In a [2014 study in Therapeutic Advances in Urology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24883105/), 40 men with lifelong PE and a baseline time to ejaculation of one minute or less did 12 weeks of pelvic floor rehabilitation. **33 of 40 (82.5%) gained control**, with mean time to ejaculation reaching about 146 seconds.
- In a [2018 long-term follow-up in the Asian Journal of Andrology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29974885/), 111 of 122 men who completed the program gained control. Three years later, **56% still had satisfactory control**.

Note that these programs included physiotherapy, electrical stimulation and biofeedback, not only home kegels.

## Step-by-step: kegel exercises for premature ejaculation

### Step 1: Find the right muscle

Next time you urinate, slow or stop the stream midway for a second. The muscle that does that is your pelvic floor. Do this only to identify it — do not make a habit of stopping your stream.

A second check: lie down and imagine you are trying to stop passing gas while also lifting your penis slightly. You should feel a lift and tightening between your scrotum and anus, with no clenching of your buttocks, thighs or abs.

### Step 2: Learn the full release

Before building strength, learn to let go. Lie on your back with knees bent. Breathe in slowly through your nose and let your pelvic floor soften and drop, as if it is gently widening. This is sometimes called a reverse kegel.

Spend two minutes on this. For premature ejaculation, **the release is as important as the squeeze**.

### Step 3: Slow holds

1. Exhale and contract the pelvic floor to about 70% effort.
2. Hold for 5 seconds while breathing normally.
3. Release completely for 5-10 seconds.
4. Repeat 10 times.

Build to 10-second holds over three to four weeks.

### Step 4: Quick flicks

1. Contract firmly for one second.
2. Release fully for one to two seconds.
3. Repeat 10-15 times.

These train the fast-twitch fibres that fire during the ejaculatory reflex.

### Step 5: Practise control during arousal

After three to four weeks of solo training, bring the skill into arousal. During masturbation, notice your arousal on a scale of 1 to 10. Keep your pelvic floor relaxed and your breathing slow through the middle of that range.

When you reach about 7, stop stimulation, take a slow breath, and do one firm pelvic floor contraction for a few seconds. Wait until arousal drops, then continue. This pairs well with the [stop-start method](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation) and the [squeeze method](/blog/squeeze-method-premature-ejaculation).

## Building the habit

The clinical programs ran three sessions a week for 12 weeks under supervision. At home, short and frequent works better than long and occasional.

A realistic daily plan:

- **Morning:** 2 minutes of breathing and full release
- **Midday:** 10 slow holds and 10 quick flicks, seated
- **Evening:** repeat the midday set lying down

That is under 10 minutes a day. The hard part is not the effort — it is remembering to do it for 12 weeks straight.

## Common mistakes

**Only squeezing.** Some men with PE already carry a tense pelvic floor. Every contraction should be followed by a full, equal-length release.

**Clenching everything.** Squeezing your glutes, thighs or abs means the pelvic floor is not doing the work. Put a hand on your belly and keep it soft.

**Holding your breath.** Breath-holding increases pressure and tension. Keep breathing throughout every rep.

**Overtraining.** Hundreds of kegels a day will not speed results. A tired muscle is harder to control, not easier.

## What the evidence does not say

Kegels are a strong, low-risk option, but they are not magic. A [2026 meta-analysis in the Journal of Sexual Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42132359/) pooled five randomised trials and found that exercise programs on their own improved time to ejaculation less than dapoxetine or combined approaches.

In Pastore's earlier randomised comparison, 57% of men in the rehabilitation group gained control, while dapoxetine produced longer average times. The takeaway is realistic expectations: pelvic floor training helps many men, works best when combined with behavioural techniques and breathing, and has none of the side effects of medication.

If PE is causing real distress, or you want to discuss options such as [PE medication](/blog/pe-medication), speak to your doctor. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ejaculation-problems/) also lists psychological and relationship factors that are worth addressing alongside physical training.

## Common questions

**Are kegels better for lifelong or acquired PE?** The Pastore studies focused on lifelong PE. Men whose PE started later in life should rule out causes such as erectile difficulties or prostate inflammation.

**Can I do kegels if I also have ED?** Yes. The same muscles support erection rigidity, which is why training often helps both. Our [kegels for men](/blog/kegels-for-men) guide covers the erection side.

**Do I need a device or biofeedback?** Not to start. Biofeedback was part of the clinical programs, but most men can learn the muscle with the steps above.

## The takeaway

Kegel exercises for premature ejaculation work by building awareness, relaxation and deliberate control of the muscles that drive the ejaculatory reflex. Train the release as much as the squeeze, keep sessions short and daily, bring the skill into arousal after a few weeks, and give it a full 12 weeks.
