Night time incontinence in adults is more common than most people admit, mostly because nobody talks about it. Waking up wet is embarrassing, but it is also a symptom with identifiable causes, and a large share of them can be treated or at least managed.

The NHS describes [urinary incontinence](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urinary-incontinence/) as a common problem that is often manageable and sometimes curable, and the same applies when it only happens at night. The first step is working out which kind you have.

## Why It Happens at Night

Two things change when you sleep. Your brain normally turns down urine production overnight, and you rely on a bladder signal strong enough to wake you. If either fails, or if the muscles that hold urine in relax too much, you leak. These are the usual suspects.

### Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea can drive bedwetting in adults, and it is often missed. A 2025 case series in _Medicine_ (Zhou et al.) described four obese men aged 27 to 40 whose bedwetting began as obstructive sleep apnea got worse. After treatment with positive airway pressure, **all four had complete remission or a drop of more than 90% in episodes**. Four patients is a small sample, so this does not tell you how common the link is. It does tell you to mention snoring, gasping, and daytime fatigue to your doctor.

### Overactive Bladder and Urge Leakage

If your bladder contracts when it should not, the urge can arrive faster than you wake. Our guides on [what causes overactive bladder](/blog/what-causes-overactive-bladder) and [urge incontinence](/blog/urge-incontinence) cover this in depth.

### Prostate and Bladder Emptying Problems

An enlarged prostate can stop the bladder emptying fully, so it fills and overflows at night. See [overflow incontinence](/blog/overflow-incontinence) for the pattern.

### Overnight Urine Volume

Drinking a lot in the evening, particularly alcohol and caffeine, raises volume and irritates the bladder. Some conditions, including diabetes, also increase urine production. Waking to pee is covered in [nocturia treatment](/blog/nocturia-treatment).

### Weak Pelvic Floor Muscles

When the pelvic floor is weak, the sphincter supporting muscles hold less well, especially when the body is relaxed. This is common after prostate surgery. In the MAPS trials, funded by the UK's National Institute for Health Research and published in _Health Technology Assessment_ (Glazener et al., 2011), men with incontinence six weeks after prostate surgery were randomised to pelvic floor muscle training and bladder training with a specialist therapist or to standard management, with continence at 12 months as the main measure.

## How to Work Out Your Cause

1. **Keep a bladder diary for three days.** Record what you drink, when you pee, and any leaks.
2. **Note the pattern.** Leaks right at the urge point suggest urgency. Constant dribbling suggests overflow. Leaks only when asleep deeply may point to apnea or high overnight output.
3. **Ask about sleep.** Snoring, witnessed pauses, or morning headaches matter.
4. **Get a basic work-up.** A urine test, blood sugar, and a prostate check are standard starting points.

## What You Can Do Tonight

- **Front-load fluids.** Drink earlier in the day and taper in the two to three hours before bed.
- **Cut evening alcohol and caffeine.** Both irritate the bladder and increase output. For more, see [foods that irritate the bladder](/blog/foods-that-irritate-the-bladder).
- **Double-void before bed.** Pee, wait a minute, then try again to empty fully.
- **Protect the bed.** Washable mattress covers and absorbent pads take the stress out of the night while you investigate.

## Training the Pelvic Floor

If your doctor agrees pelvic floor weakness is part of the picture, build a daily routine. Learn the correct muscle in [our male kegel guide](/blog/male-kegel-exercises), then follow a structured plan like [bladder training exercises](/blog/bladder-training-exercises). Expect to give it about three months before judging results, and combine it with the habit changes above.

If your muscles are tight rather than weak, squeezing harder makes things worse. Check [how to tell if your pelvic floor is tight or weak](/blog/how-to-tell-if-pelvic-floor-is-tight-or-weak) first.

## What Progress Looks Like

Fewer wet nights per week is the first sign. Aim for steady improvement rather than perfection, and review your bladder diary monthly. If nothing changes after a few months of consistent effort, return to your doctor. There are medications, devices, and procedures worth discussing. The [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/bladder-control-problems) also publishes plain-language guidance on bladder control problems.
