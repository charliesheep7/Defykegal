Search for the best exercise for penis growth and you will find routines promising inches in weeks. None of them come with a study, because no exercise has been shown to add tissue to an adult penis.

That is the honest part. The useful part is that "size" is not only about tissue. How full and hard an erection gets, how much length sits hidden behind body fat, and how well blood reaches the penis all change what you see — and all of those respond to training.

So here is the research on what works, what is a waste of time, and what can actually do damage.

## Why the penis does not grow like a muscle

The penis is not built like your biceps. The shaft is made of two columns of spongy erectile tissue, the corpora cavernosa, wrapped in a tough fibrous sheath called the tunica albuginea. An erection happens when those spaces fill with blood and the sheath pulls tight.

Skeletal muscle grows because training creates small injuries that heal thicker. Erectile tissue does not work that way. When it is injured, it tends to heal with **scar tissue**, which is stiffer and less able to expand — the opposite of what you want.

What does surround the penis is skeletal muscle. The ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus muscles sit in the pelvic floor, and during an erection they squeeze the base of the penis to keep blood from draining out. Those muscles respond to training exactly like any other.

That distinction explains everything below.

## What the research actually supports

### 1. Pelvic floor training for fuller erections

This is the strongest evidence in the whole topic. A randomised controlled trial by Dorey and colleagues in the [British Journal of General Practice](https://bjgp.org/content/54/508/819) put 55 men with erectile dysfunction through pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback.

**After six months, 40% had regained normal erectile function and another 34.5% had improved.** At three months, the exercise group scored 6.74 points higher on the erectile function scale than the control group.

A firmer erection is a fuller one. For many men, the penis they see at their best is simply a better-pressurised version of the same organ.

### 2. Aerobic exercise for blood flow

Erections are a vascular event, and cardio is vascular training. A 2017 meta-analysis by Silva and colleagues in the [British Journal of Sports Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27707739/) pooled seven randomised trials with 478 men with erectile dysfunction.

Exercise improved erectile function scores by an average of **3.85 points**, and the authors pointed to moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise as the most effective type. Brisk running, cycling or rowing counts. For more on this side of things, see our guide to [increasing blood flow to the penis naturally](/blog/how-to-increase-blood-flow-to-penis-naturally).

### 3. Fat loss to reveal hidden length

A fat pad sits over the pubic bone at the base of the penis. As it thickens, it covers part of the shaft, so less of the penis is visible and usable. Losing that fat does not grow anything — it uncovers length that was already there.

This is the closest thing to "exercise for penis growth" that is anatomically real, and for men carrying extra weight around the middle it is often the biggest visible change on offer.

## What does not work, and what can hurt

**Jelqing.** The repeated pulling motion is supposed to create microtears that heal bigger. The [Sexual Medicine Society of North America](https://www.smsna.org/patients/did-you-know/what-is-jelqing-and-does-it-actually-work) states there is no scientific evidence it permanently increases size, and lists pain, bruising and scar tissue among the risks, along with a possible link to Peyronie disease and erectile dysfunction.

That scarring risk is not hypothetical. [Peyronie disease](/blog/peyronie-disease) is a plaque of scar tissue in the tunica that bends and can shorten the penis. Forceful manipulation is also one of the things men with [hard flaccid syndrome](/blog/hard-flaccid-syndrome) often report beforehand.

**Pills and creams.** No oral supplement has been shown to change penis size. We cover the claims in detail in our review of [penis growth tablets](/blog/pennis-growth-tablet).

**Traction devices** sit in between. They are the only non-surgical method with measured length gains, but those gains are small and demanding. In a 2009 study by Gontero and colleagues in the [Journal of Sexual Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19138361/), men with Peyronie disease wore an extender for **at least five hours a day for six months**. Stretched length rose by an average of 1.3 cm. That is real, but it is modest, it required hours of daily wear, and it was measured in a specific clinical group.

## A practical routine that targets what can change

Here is how to put the evidence into a week.

1. **Find the right muscles.** Next time you urinate, briefly slow the stream. The muscles doing that are your pelvic floor. Do not make a habit of stopping mid-stream — use it only to identify the squeeze. You should also feel the base of the penis lift slightly.
2. **Train slow holds.** Contract and hold for five seconds, then fully relax for five seconds. Build to 10 repetitions.
3. **Add quick squeezes.** Contract hard for one second, then release. Ten in a row. These train the fast-twitch fibres that clamp down during arousal.
4. **Repeat three times a day.** Lying down is easiest at first. Progress to sitting, then standing.
5. **Keep breathing and keep other muscles loose.** Your glutes, thighs and stomach should stay relaxed. If they are straining, the pelvic floor is not doing the work.
6. **Do cardio most days.** Aim for around 150 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise a week, broken up however suits you.
7. **Cut body fat gradually.** Consistent training plus a modest calorie deficit reduces the pubic fat pad over months, not days.

If you want more variety once the basics feel easy, work through [kegel exercises for men](/blog/7-kegel-exercises-for-men) for progressions.

## Building the habit

Pelvic floor training fails for the same reason most training fails: people stop. There is no mirror feedback, the muscles are invisible, and progress is slow enough that it is easy to assume nothing is happening.

The Dorey trial assessed men at three and six months. That is the timescale to think in. A short daily session done for 12 weeks beats an intense week followed by nothing, because muscle adapts to frequency.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing length with force.** Pulling, hanging weights or aggressive stretching carries a real risk of scarring and curvature for little or no benefit.
- **Squeezing the wrong muscles.** Clenching the buttocks or holding your breath means the pelvic floor is not being trained.
- **Never relaxing.** A pelvic floor that is always tight can cause pain and worse erections. Full relaxation between reps matters as much as the squeeze.
- **Measuring every few days.** Nothing measurable happens that fast, and the checking feeds anxiety. Judge after three months.

## Is your size actually the problem?

Worth a direct look before anything else. A 2015 systematic review by Veale and colleagues in [BJU International](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25487360/) pooled measurements from up to 15,521 men taken by health professionals. **The average erect length was 13.12 cm — about 5.2 inches.** Average flaccid length was 9.16 cm.

Many men who worry about size are well within that range, and the view looking down makes any penis look shorter than it is. Our breakdown of [average penis length by age](/blog/average-penis-length-by-age) goes further.

If size worry is affecting sex or your mood, talking to a doctor or sex therapist is a genuinely useful step — not a sign something is wrong with you.

## Common questions

**How long until I notice a difference?** Pelvic floor and cardio changes to erection quality typically show up over about three months. Fat loss depends on how much you have to lose.

**Can exercise shrink my penis?** No. But weight gain can hide more of it, and poor cardiovascular health can make erections less full.

**Should I see a doctor?** Yes, if you have curvature, pain, a lump in the shaft, or erections that have noticeably weakened. Those need an assessment, not a workout.

## The short version

The best exercise for penis growth does not grow the penis. It makes the erection harder and fuller, improves blood flow, and uncovers length hidden by fat. Pelvic floor training has the strongest evidence, aerobic exercise helps blood flow, and fat loss changes what you can see.

Skip jelqing. Train the muscles that do the real work.
