## What the Stop-Start Method Can and Cannot Do

Stop-start is a behavioral technique for practicing awareness of arousal before ejaculation. It may help some people, but evidence does not establish a reliable response, a fixed timeline, or a permanent effect for everyone.

Premature ejaculation can have several contributing factors. The [European Association of Urology (EAU) guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation) recommends assessing the symptoms and any underlying causes before choosing a treatment approach.

## What the Stop-Start Method Actually Is

The basic idea is to pause stimulation before ejaculation, wait for arousal to drop, and then resume. The [NHS description of the stop-go technique](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ejaculation-problems/) places it among techniques a psychosexual therapist may teach.

The aim is to notice sensations as arousal rises and practice pausing before ejaculation feels inevitable. This describes the technique, not a promise that it will change an underlying cause or produce lasting control.

The [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation) says evidence for psychosexual interventions used alone is weak and inconsistent, and their long-term outcomes are unknown. A clinician or psychosexual therapist can help discuss whether behavioral practice, medication, or combined care fits the situation.

## What the Evidence Shows

Behavioral techniques take practice, but a study of a technique does not guarantee the same result for everyone who tries it.

A [2023 quasi-experimental study of 80 men](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37561708/) compared stop-start alone with stop-start plus sphincter-control training. The combined group improved more on several measures at three and six months, but the authors described the between-group effects as **clinically weak**. This study does not establish that adding a general kegel program produces a meaningful or lasting benefit for every person. The [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation) also notes uncertainty about long-term outcomes of psychosexual interventions.

## How to Actually Do the Stop-Start Method

The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ejaculation-problems/) describes practicing the pause during self-stimulation and, when appropriate, during partnered sex. The sequence is:

1. **Notice approaching climax.** Pay attention to the sensations before ejaculation feels inevitable.
2. **Pause stimulation.** Wait for the urge to fade.
3. **Resume if comfortable.** The pause-and-resume sequence can be repeated.

If practicing with a partner, communicate about when to pause and resume. There is no evidence-based number of seconds, cycles, or weekly sessions that guarantees an outcome for everyone.

## What Progress Looks Like

There is no reliable timetable for improvement. The [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation) describes mixed evidence for behavioral methods used alone and says their long-term outcomes are unknown. Persistent or distressing symptoms warrant discussion with a clinician or psychosexual therapist.

Changes in symptoms can have several causes, so practice alone cannot identify why premature ejaculation is occurring.

## Common Questions

**Can I use stop-start and pelvic floor exercises together?**
Some studies have explored combining them. In the [2023 comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37561708/), the combined group had statistically better results, but the between-group differences were described as clinically weak. A clinician can help assess whether pelvic floor training is suitable.

**Do I need a partner to practice?**
No. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ejaculation-problems/) describes the technique during self-stimulation and says it may later be tried during partnered sex.

**Will this fix things permanently?**
There is no guarantee of a permanent result; long-term outcomes of behavioral interventions remain uncertain in the [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation). If premature ejaculation is persistent, distressing, or started suddenly, speak with a clinician about possible contributing causes.

## The Bottom Line

Stop-start offers a way to practice noticing arousal and pausing stimulation. It may help some people, but available studies do not show that it is reliably effective alone or that pairing it with pelvic floor exercises is best for everyone. The [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/disorders-of-ejaculation) recommends considering the type of premature ejaculation and possible underlying causes.

If symptoms continue or cause distress, a clinician or psychosexual therapist can discuss the available options.
