Most men searching for a kegel PDF want one thing: a plan on a page. Not the anatomy lecture, not the history — the actual hold times, rep counts and weekly progression, laid out so it can be followed without thinking.

That is what this is. The progression below follows the structure used in the clinical trials that produced the results men are chasing. Print it, screenshot it, or copy the table. It works the same either way.

## Before Week 1: Find the Muscle

Skip this and the entire plan trains the wrong thing, which is the single most common reason kegels "do not work."

The muscles you want are the ones that lift the base of the penis and pull the testicles slightly upward. Two ways to locate them:

1. **The urine test, once only.** Mid-stream, stop the flow. That is the contraction. Do not make a habit of this — repeatedly interrupting urination can irritate the bladder.
2. **The mirror test.** Stand naked in front of a mirror and try to lift the base of the penis without moving anything else. If the penis rises slightly and the scrotum tightens, you found it.

What should **not** happen: buttocks squeezing, thighs tensing, abdominals bracing, or holding your breath. Those are the compensations that make men think they are training when they are not.

## Why This Protocol Rather Than Any Other

The numbers below are not invented. They mirror the structure of the trials that produced measurable outcomes.

Dorey and colleagues randomised men with erectile dysfunction to pelvic floor muscle exercises versus lifestyle advice, publishing in *BJU International* in 2004. At six months, **40% had regained normal erectile function and a further 35.5% had improved significantly**. A [2025 narrative review in the International Journal of Impotence Research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41443-025-01034-5) reached similar conclusions across erectile and ejaculatory function.

On the urinary side, [Van Kampen and colleagues published a randomised controlled trial in *The Lancet* in 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10675166/): after three months, **88% of men doing pelvic floor re-education after radical prostatectomy were continent versus 56% of controls**. A [2019 randomised controlled trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6858748/) reported the same direction of effect on pelvic floor function and incontinence.

Two features are common to every protocol that worked: **progressive hold time** and **daily frequency**. Neither is optional.

## The 12-Week Plan

Three sessions a day, spread out — morning, midday, evening. Rest between reps should equal the hold time.

| Weeks | Hold | Rest | Reps per set | Sets per day | Quick flicks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | 3 sec | 3 sec | 5 | 3 | — |
| 3–4 | 5 sec | 5 sec | 8 | 3 | 10 per day |
| 5–6 | 7 sec | 7 sec | 10 | 3 | 2 × 10 |
| 7–8 | 10 sec | 10 sec | 10 | 3 | 2 × 10 |
| 9–12 | 10 sec | 5 sec | 12 | 3 | 3 × 10 |

**Quick flicks** are one-second maximum contractions with a full release between each. They train the fast-twitch fibres responsible for the reflexive contractions during ejaculation and the ones that stop leakage when you cough or lift.

**The rule that matters more than the table:** if the hold gets shaky, sloppy, or recruits your glutes, drop back a row. A clean 5-second hold beats a compensated 10-second one every time.

## Four Mistakes That Waste the 12 Weeks

1. **Squeezing everything.** Glutes, abs and thighs joining in means the pelvic floor is doing less work than you think. Put a hand on your abdomen — it should stay soft.
2. **Holding your breath.** Breathe normally through every rep. Breath-holding creates downward pressure that works against the contraction.
3. **Never fully releasing.** The relaxation phase is half the exercise. A muscle that never returns to baseline gets tight rather than strong, and a tight pelvic floor produces its own symptoms — see [hypertonic pelvic floor](/blog/hypertonic-pelvic-floor) if training makes things worse instead of better.
4. **Doing them only during sex.** Twelve weeks of daily training is what changes the muscle. Using them in the moment is the payoff, not the training.

## What Progress Looks Like

- **Weeks 2–4:** The contraction gets easier to find and hold. No visible change yet, and that is exactly on schedule.
- **Weeks 4–6:** Urinary control usually improves first — less post-void dribble, better response to urgency.
- **Weeks 6–8:** Erection firmness starts changing. Morning erections are the most honest signal because they bypass psychology entirely.
- **Week 12:** The trial timeline. Trained consistently, this is where the difference stops being ambiguous.
- **Symptoms worse, not better:** Stop and reassess. That usually means a tight pelvic floor being trained as if it were a weak one.

## After Week 12

Maintenance is one set a day, three to four days a week, at the week 9–12 numbers. That holds the adaptation without the daily load.

Progression from there is not more reps — it is harder positions. Standing is harder than sitting. Contractions held during a squat or a lunge are harder still. And for men chasing ejaculatory control specifically, [the stop-start method](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation) pairs naturally with a trained pelvic floor.
