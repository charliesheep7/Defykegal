Most men never think about the tip of the penis until something looks or feels off. Then it is all they can think about.

**The tip, or glans, is the most sensitive part of the penis and the part most likely to show early signs of irritation or infection.** Here is what it is made of, what normal looks like, how to look after it, and which symptoms deserve a doctor's eyes.

## What the Tip of the Penis Actually Is

The medical name is the glans penis. [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/mens-health/glans-penis) describes the penis as having three parts: the root, the shaft and the glans. You may also hear the glans called the head or the tip.

The glans is not a separate organ. According to [Kenhub](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/glans-penis), it is the expanded end of the corpus spongiosum, the column of erectile tissue that runs along the underside of the shaft and surrounds the urethra. It caps the ends of the two larger erectile chambers, the corpora cavernosa.

A few landmarks are worth knowing:

- **Meatus.** The opening at the very tip, where urine and semen leave the body.
- **Corona.** The rounded rim at the base of the glans.
- **Neck.** The narrower groove just behind the corona that separates the glans from the shaft.
- **Foreskin.** In uncircumcised men, a loose double layer of skin that covers the glans.
- **Frenulum.** A thin band of tissue on the underside that anchors the foreskin to the glans.

## What the Glans Does

The glans has two jobs, and both run through the meatus. When the penis is soft, urine passes out through it. During orgasm, semen does.

It also plays a quieter role in sex. Healthline notes that while the shaft is rigid during an erection, the glans itself stays softer, which helps it act as a shock absorber.

Then there is sensation. **The glans has a high concentration of nerve endings, which makes it the most sensitive part of the penis.** That sensitivity feeds the ejaculation reflex. Healthline also notes that some studies link heightened sensitivity of the glans and shaft to premature ejaculation in some men. We come back to that below.

## What a Healthy Tip Looks Like

There is no single correct look. The glans is often described as cone, mushroom or acorn shaped, and the word glans means acorn in Latin. Its size and shape vary from man to man. Some men have a large, bulbous glans and others a narrower one.

A few general signs of a healthy tip:

- **Smooth skin.** Healthline notes that a wrinkly or scaly look can point to dryness or irritation.
- **Even color.** The color is whatever is normal for you. Watch for changes rather than comparing with anyone else.
- **Small bumps around the rim can be normal.** Pearly penile papules are benign growths that usually appear as small bumps on or around the corona. Healthline describes them as noncancerous.

## Common Problems at the Tip

### Balanitis

Balanitis is the most common problem that affects the glans. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/balanitis/) describes it as the head of the penis being swollen and sore. It is not usually serious, but it is worth getting checked so the cause can be found.

Typical symptoms include a swollen, itchy, sore glans that usually looks red, though redness can be less obvious on brown or black skin. You may also notice:

- Pain when peeing
- A thick discharge from under the foreskin
- Bleeding around the foreskin
- An unpleasant smell
- Difficulty pulling the foreskin back

Treatment depends on the cause. The NHS says a GP may prescribe a mild steroid cream, an antifungal cream or antibiotics. If your symptoms are severe, they may also check for underlying conditions such as diabetes.

### Tight Foreskin

A tight foreskin, or phimosis, makes it hard to pull the skin back off the glans. The [NHS page on phimosis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/phimosis/) says it is normal in young boys but can affect adults too. It becomes a problem when it causes swelling, pain when peeing, bleeding, discharge or painful erections that make sex difficult.

### Cuts and Irritation

The skin of the glans is thin, so friction from sex or masturbation can leave it sore or nicked. Our guide to a [cut on the penis](/blog/cut-on-penis) covers how to care for minor injuries and when to get help.

## How to Keep the Tip Healthy

Good care is simple. The NHS advice for men with balanitis works well as a daily routine for anyone:

1. **Wash every day with water.** Gently pull back the foreskin if you have one and rinse the glans with warm water.
2. **Skip soap and shower gel on the glans.** The NHS advises against them during balanitis, and against perfumed soap, talcum powder and deodorants on the penis because they can irritate it.
3. **Dry gently.** Pat the area dry after washing instead of rubbing.
4. **Pick gentle condoms.** If condoms irritate you, choose ones made for sensitive skin.
5. **Re-cover the glans after cleaning.** The NHS advises always bringing the foreskin back over the head once you have washed.

Keeping the area clean and dry removes the main trigger for irritation. For more everyday habits, see our full list of [penis health tips](/blog/penis-health-tips).

## When the Tip Is Too Sensitive

Plenty of men worry less about how the glans looks and more about how fast it gets them to the finish line. Numbing sprays dull the glans, but they also dull the pleasure. Pelvic floor training works on a different part of the system.

The pelvic floor muscles run the ejaculation reflex. Training them gives you more conscious control over that reflex, instead of trying to reduce feeling at the tip.

The research is small but encouraging:

- In a [2014 study in Therapeutic Advances in Urology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24883105/), Pastore and colleagues gave 40 men with lifelong premature ejaculation 12 weeks of pelvic floor muscle rehabilitation. **33 of the 40 men, or 82.5 percent, gained control of their ejaculatory reflex.** Their average time to ejaculation rose to about 146 seconds, from a starting point of one minute or less.
- In an earlier [randomized study in the International Journal of Andrology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22320846/), the same team compared pelvic floor rehabilitation with the drug dapoxetine. After 12 weeks, 11 of 19 men in the rehabilitation group could control the reflex. The drug groups had somewhat higher response rates, but the authors noted that rehabilitation was much cheaper.

## When to See a Doctor

The NHS advises seeing a GP if the head of your penis is swollen, itchy, sore or red, or if balanitis treatment has not worked. Adults can also go to a sexual health clinic, and many offer walk-in appointments.

Get checked promptly if you notice:

- Sores, ulcers or a rash on the glans
- Discharge, bleeding or a bad smell
- Pain or difficulty when peeing
- A foreskin you cannot pull back, or one that is stuck behind the glans
- Any lump or patch that changes, grows or does not heal

The NHS lists thrush and sexually transmitted infections such as gonorrhoea and chlamydia among the causes of balanitis, so a test is the fastest way to know what you are dealing with.

## The Bottom Line

The tip of the penis is the most sensitive and most exposed part of the organ. A healthy glans can look many ways, and small even bumps around the rim are often normal. Wash daily with water, skip harsh products and get any redness, swelling, sores or discharge checked. If sensitivity is costing you control, pelvic floor training is a low-risk way to build it back.
