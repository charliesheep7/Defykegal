Most advice on lasting longer in bed is either a numbing product or a distraction trick. Both treat ejaculation as something that happens to you. It is not — ejaculation is a reflex with a muscular component you can train, which is why the home remedy with the best research behind it is not a cream or a supplement. It is a daily exercise that takes three minutes.

Here is what works at home, ranked by the strength of the evidence.

## Why Timing Is Trainable in the First Place

Ejaculation happens in two stages. First, emission: seminal fluid collects in the urethra. Then expulsion: the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles of the pelvic floor contract rhythmically to push it out. Those pelvic floor muscles are skeletal muscle — the same category as your biceps — which means they respond to training and, crucially, to voluntary control.

Men who last longer are usually doing two things well: they recognise arousal levels before the point of no return, and they can consciously hold a pelvic floor contraction to delay the reflex. Both are learnable skills.

Before anything else, calibrate your expectations. Research measuring intravaginal ejaculatory latency time across thousands of couples puts the median near five to six minutes, not the twenty or thirty minutes that pornography suggests. The [NHS guidance on ejaculation problems](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ejaculation-problems/) frames premature ejaculation as a persistent pattern that causes distress, not an occasional fast finish.

## Home Remedy 1: Pelvic Floor Training

This is the one with real clinical numbers behind it.

In 2014, Antonio Pastore and colleagues published a study in [Therapeutic Advances in Urology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4003840/) following 40 men aged 19 to 46 with lifelong premature ejaculation. All had a baseline ejaculatory latency of 60 seconds or less, averaging **31.7 seconds**. After a structured pelvic floor rehabilitation programme, average latency reached **124.6 seconds at six weeks and 146.2 seconds at twelve weeks** — more than a fourfold increase. Thirty-three of the 40 men, **82.5 percent**, gained control of their ejaculatory reflex, with no adverse effects reported.

Here is the routine:

1. **Find the muscle.** Squeeze as if stopping urine mid-flow while lifting your testicles. Abs, glutes and thighs stay relaxed. If you cannot tell whether you are doing it right, our guide on [how to tell if your pelvic floor is tight or weak](/blog/how-to-tell-if-pelvic-floor-is-tight-or-weak) walks through the self-checks.
2. **Slow holds.** Squeeze for 5 seconds, release fully for 5 seconds. Ten reps.
3. **Quick flicks.** Ten fast contractions with no hold. These train the fast-twitch fibres you actually use during sex.
4. **Three rounds a day.** Morning, afternoon, evening. Under three minutes total.
5. **Progress weekly.** Add a second to each hold until you reach 10 seconds clean.

One warning: if you already have pelvic pain, difficulty starting urination, or a sense of constant tension down there, more squeezing will make things worse. That pattern points to a [hypertonic pelvic floor](/blog/hypertonic-pelvic-floor), which needs relaxation work first.

## Home Remedy 2: The Stop-Start and Squeeze Methods

These behavioural techniques train arousal awareness — the second half of the equation.

**Stop-start**: during masturbation or sex, build to the point just before inevitability, then stop completely for 30 seconds until the urge subsides. Resume. Repeat three times, then finish on the fourth. Do this two or three times a week and the threshold shifts.

**Squeeze**: same build-up, but at the near point your partner or you squeeze firmly just below the head of the penis for several seconds until the urge passes. It is blunter than stop-start and some men find it kills the mood, but it works fast.

Our detailed walkthroughs of the [stop-start method](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation) and the [squeeze method](/blog/squeeze-method-premature-ejaculation) cover the mechanics in full. Used alongside pelvic floor training, these two approaches reinforce each other: the muscles get stronger while your read on arousal gets sharper.

## Home Remedy 3: Breathing and Arousal Pacing

Fast, shallow chest breathing drives sympathetic nervous system activity — the branch that triggers ejaculation. Slow diaphragmatic breathing pulls you the other way.

Practise this outside the bedroom first: four seconds in through the nose, letting your belly expand, six seconds out through the mouth. Five minutes a day. During sex, return to that rhythm whenever arousal spikes. It is not a gimmick; it is the same autonomic lever used in anxiety treatment, and performance anxiety is a real accelerator for many men.

Pair it with pacing: change tempo or position before you reach 8 out of 10 arousal, not after. Most men wait until they are at 9 and then try to slam the brakes.

## Home Remedy 4: The Lifestyle Variables That Matter

Some of these are unglamorous, but they move the needle:

- **Cardiovascular exercise.** Thirty minutes most days improves endothelial function and erection quality, which reduces the rushing that comes from worrying about losing an erection.
- **Less alcohol.** It reduces control more than it delays finishing, and it softens erections. Our piece on [whether alcohol causes ED](/blog/does-alcohol-cause-ed) has the evidence.
- **Sleep.** Under six hours drops testosterone and raises anxiety. Both work against you.
- **Masturbation timing.** Masturbating an hour or two before sex genuinely extends the second round for some men. It is a crude tool, but it is free.

## What Does Not Work

Save your money on most "stamina" supplements. Herbal blends marketed for lasting longer are largely unregulated, rarely tested in controlled trials, and occasionally contain undeclared prescription drugs. Healthline's overview of [approaches to lasting longer](https://www.healthline.com/health/mens-health/how-to-last-longer-in-bed) makes the same point about evidence quality.

Numbing sprays and thick condoms do reduce sensation and can extend time, and they are reasonably safe. Treat them as a bridge while you train, not a permanent solution — long-term reliance on reduced sensation tends to create erection problems of its own.

## Realistic Timeline

**Weeks 1 to 2**: you can find and hold the pelvic floor contraction reliably. Little change in bed yet.

**Weeks 3 to 6**: control improves noticeably. The Pastore group had already more than tripled their average latency by week six.

**Weeks 8 to 12**: the behavioural and muscular gains compound. This is where most men reach a time they are happy with.

If nothing has shifted after three months of consistent work, see a doctor. Premature ejaculation sometimes has a treatable underlying driver — thyroid problems, prostatitis, or an erection problem that is causing the rushing — and there are prescription options worth discussing.
