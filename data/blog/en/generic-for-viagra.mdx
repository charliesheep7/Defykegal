The generic for Viagra is **sildenafil citrate**. Same molecule, same dose, same effect, and historically about one twentieth of the price.

If that is all you came for, you have it. What follows is the part that actually changes decisions: what "generic" legally guarantees, what the price gap really looks like, how to avoid the counterfeit market, and the specific failure mode that no version of this pill can fix.

## What Generic Actually Means

A generic drug is not a copy in the loose sense. It is the same active ingredient, at the same strength, in the same form, taken the same way — and it must prove **bioequivalence** to the original before approval.

Bioequivalence means the generic delivers the same quantity of drug into the bloodstream at the same rate, within a tight statistical margin. That is a pharmacokinetic standard, not a marketing claim. A tablet that fails it does not reach the market.

What legitimately differs:

- **Shape, colour, and markings** — trade dress stays with the brand, so generics cannot be blue diamonds
- **Inactive ingredients** — binders, coatings, and dyes vary between manufacturers
- **Price** — the reason any of this matters

Nothing about the sildenafil itself changes. Men who report that the generic "feels weaker" are almost always comparing across different circumstances — a heavier meal, more alcohol, more stress — rather than across different chemistry.

## Why It Got So Cheap

Pfizer's Viagra patent position in the US ran until April 2020, but Teva reached a settlement allowing an earlier launch and began selling generic sildenafil in December 2017 with a period of exclusivity. Once that window closed, more than ten manufacturers entered and competed on price.

The result was the classic patent-cliff collapse. The average wholesale cost of a 100 mg tablet went from around **62 dollars branded to under 3 dollars generic**. Retail pricing is messier — it depends on your pharmacy, your insurance, and whether you use a discount card — but the gap has stayed enormous.

Practical notes on cost:

- **Ask about tablet splitting.** A 100 mg tablet often costs the same as a 50 mg one. Splitting is common practice with scored tablets, but confirm with the pharmacist rather than assuming.
- **Prices vary wildly between pharmacies** for the identical generic. Comparing is worth real money here.
- **Telehealth ED services** bundle a consultation with supply. Convenient, but check whether you are paying a subscription premium over a local pharmacy filling the same generic.

## The Other Generics Worth Knowing

Sildenafil is not the only PDE5 inhibitor whose patent has expired. The full generic shelf:

- **Sildenafil** (was Viagra) — onset around 30 to 60 minutes, active for roughly 4 to 6 hours. Food, especially fatty food, delays absorption noticeably.
- **Tadalafil** (was Cialis) — onset slower, but active up to 36 hours, and available as a low daily dose that removes the timing question entirely. Food has much less effect.
- **Vardenafil** (was Levitra) — broadly similar to sildenafil in profile.
- **Avanafil** (was Stendra) — fastest onset of the group, roughly 15 to 30 minutes, though generic availability is patchier.

A [network meta-analysis of 179 randomised trials covering 50,620 patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23395275/) found all of them significantly better than placebo, with differences between them modest compared with the difference between any of them and nothing. The choice between drugs is usually about timing, side-effect tolerance, and cost rather than raw potency.

The [NHS guidance on sildenafil](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/sildenafil-viagra/) is a good plain-language reference on dosing and interactions if you want a non-commercial source.

## The Counterfeit Problem

This is where the money saved can cost you.

Erectile dysfunction drugs are among the most counterfeited medicines in the world, precisely because demand is high and embarrassment keeps buyers away from pharmacies. Seized counterfeits have been found containing wrong doses, no active ingredient at all, or undeclared substances entirely.

Simple rules that keep you out of it:

1. **No prescription and no consultation means no legitimacy** in markets where the drug is prescription-only. A site that ships on a credit card alone is not operating legally.
2. **Buy from a licensed pharmacy** — a physical one, a recognised chain's online arm, or a telehealth service that connects you to an actual prescriber.
3. **Be suspicious of "herbal Viagra."** Supplements marketed for erections have repeatedly been found spiked with undeclared sildenafil or analogues, which is both illegal and dangerous if you take nitrates. We cover that market in [sex pills for men](/blog/sex-pills-for-men).
4. **Extremely low prices from unknown sellers** are a signal, not a bargain. Legitimate generic sildenafil is already cheap.

## What the Pill Does Not Do

Here is the mechanism, stripped down. Sexual stimulation releases nitric oxide in the penis, which raises cyclic GMP, which relaxes the smooth muscle in the arteries so blood floods in. An enzyme called PDE5 breaks cyclic GMP down. Sildenafil blocks PDE5, so the signal lasts longer and the arteries stay open.

Notice what that describes: **inflow**. The drug helps blood get in.

An erection also depends on blood staying in. The ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus muscles — your pelvic floor — compress the veins at the base of the penis and hold the pressure. When those muscles are weak, blood leaks back out under pressure. That is called venous leak, and it produces a very recognisable pattern: an erection that starts fine and softens, or one that never quite reaches full rigidity no matter what the arteries do.

**No PDE5 inhibitor addresses that**, which is a large part of why roughly a third of men get little benefit from them. The drug is working on a system that was not the problem.

That side is trainable. In a study by [Dorey and colleagues published in *BJU International* (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16104916/), men with erectile dysfunction who did pelvic floor muscle exercises with biofeedback were followed for six months: **40% regained normal erectile function and a further 35.5% improved.** No drug involved.

The two approaches are not rivals. Inflow and outflow are different halves of the same hydraulic system, and plenty of men get the best result by using a pill while building the muscle underneath it.

## How to Use It Sensibly

If you and a prescriber decide sildenafil is right:

1. **Take it on a relatively empty stomach.** A heavy or fatty meal can delay onset by an hour or more. Tadalafil is far less fussy if this is a recurring problem.
2. **Give it a proper window** — 30 to 60 minutes before, not five.
3. **Expect stimulation to still be required.** The drug amplifies an existing signal. It does not manufacture arousal.
4. **Try a dose at least four to eight times before writing it off.** Regulators and urologists both note that early "failures" are frequently under-trial, not non-response.
5. **Report side effects rather than tolerating them.** Headache, flushing, nasal congestion, and indigestion are common and often dose-related. Vision changes, hearing loss, or an erection lasting over four hours are emergencies.
6. **Never combine with nitrates.** This is the one absolute. If you take nitrates for angina, PDE5 inhibitors are off the table entirely.

And treat the underlying picture seriously. Erectile dysfunction is often the first visible sign of vascular disease, sometimes years ahead of cardiac symptoms. A pill that masks the symptom without anyone looking at the cause is a missed opportunity. ## Common Questions

**Is Viagra Connect the same thing?** It is sildenafil 50 mg, sold in UK pharmacies after a pharmacist consultation rather than a doctor's prescription. Same molecule, different route of access.

**Does sildenafil increase desire?** No. It affects blood flow, not libido. If desire is the problem, testosterone, mood, medication side effects, and relationship factors are the places to look — see [how to increase libido in men](/blog/how-to-increase-libido-in-men).

**Can I take it every day?** Sildenafil is designed for on-demand use. Tadalafil has a licensed low daily dose if you want the timing pressure removed.

**Will I become dependent on it?** Not physically. Psychological reliance is a real thing men report, though — one reason building genuine function underneath matters. More on that in [ED medication](/blog/ed-medication).

**Does it help premature ejaculation?** Not directly, and it is not licensed for it. Ejaculatory control responds better to pelvic floor training and behavioural methods like the [stop-start method](/blog/stop-start-method-for-premature-ejaculation).

## The Bottom Line

The generic for Viagra is sildenafil, it is pharmacologically identical, and it costs a small fraction of the brand. Get it through a legitimate pharmacy with a real consultation, keep well away from the counterfeit market, and take it correctly before deciding whether it works.

Then look at the half of the erection the pill does not touch. Inflow is chemistry. Outflow is muscle — and muscle responds to training.
