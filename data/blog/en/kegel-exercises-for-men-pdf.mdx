## Why kegels work for men

Your pelvic floor is a hammock of muscles slung between your tailbone and pubic bone. Two of them — the bulbocavernosus and ischiocavernosus — wrap around the base of the penis. When they contract, they help trap blood inside the erectile tissue and pump it upward. Stronger, more responsive muscles mean firmer erections and better control over ejaculation.

This isn't gym-bro theory. In a [randomized controlled trial led by physiotherapist Grace Dorey](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16104916/), published in *BJU International*, men who trained their pelvic floor muscles saw real change: **40% regained normal erectile function and another 35.5% improved**, while the group that only made lifestyle changes lagged behind. That's roughly three in four men getting measurably better from a muscle they'd never trained.

The same muscles govern bladder control. A landmark *Lancet* trial by Van Kampen and colleagues found pelvic floor training sped up the return of continence after prostate surgery far better than doing nothing. And for men who finish too quickly, a 2016 study by Shah and colleagues found pelvic floor training lengthened ejaculatory latency. One muscle group, several payoffs.

## The step-by-step routine

### Step 1: Find the right muscle

Before you count a single rep, make sure you're contracting the correct muscle. Two reliable ways to locate it:

1. **The stop-the-stream test.** Next time you urinate, stop the flow midway. The muscle you just used is your pelvic floor. Do this **once** to identify it — not as a regular exercise, since repeatedly interrupting urination can irritate the bladder.
2. **The gas test.** Tighten the muscle you'd use to stop yourself from passing wind. You should feel a lift and squeeze around the anus and base of the penis.

If you're doing it right, the base of your penis will draw slightly inward and your testicles will lift a little. Your stomach, thighs, and glutes should stay relaxed. Watch for that — most men accidentally clench everything but the muscle that matters.

### Step 2: Do the daily reps

Here's the core routine, in line with [Mayo Clinic's guidance for men](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/mens-health/in-depth/kegel-exercises-for-men/art-20045074) and the [step-by-step method from Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/bladder-and-bowel/step-by-step-guide-to-performing-kegel-exercises):

1. **Squeeze** the pelvic floor and hold for **3 to 5 seconds**.
2. **Relax** fully for the same amount of time — the release matters as much as the squeeze.
3. Repeat for **10 reps** — that's one set.
4. Do **4 sets across the day**, for a total of **40 kegels daily**.

As the muscle gets stronger over a few weeks, build your hold time up toward **10 seconds** per rep. Don't chase higher numbers: Mayo Clinic warns that going past 40 reps a day or holding beyond 10 seconds fatigues the muscle instead of strengthening it. More is not better here — consistent is better.

### Step 3: Mix in quick contractions

Once slow holds feel easy, add a second style: **fast squeezes**. Contract hard and release immediately, ten times in a row. These train the reflex your pelvic floor uses in the split second before ejaculation, which is exactly the control men chasing more stamina are after.

You now have everything a printout would give you. The harder part is doing it every single day.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Even with the right routine, a few errors quietly cancel your progress:

- **Holding your breath.** Breathe normally through every rep. If you're bracing your abs and holding air, you're recruiting the wrong muscles.
- **Clenching your glutes or thighs.** Isolation is everything. If your buttocks or legs are tensing, you're compensating and under-training the pelvic floor.
- **Overtraining.** Sore, fatigued pelvic muscles can actually feel tighter and perform worse. Stick to 40 quality reps, not 100 sloppy ones. If you already deal with tightness, learn [how to relax the pelvic floor muscles](/blog/how-to-relax-pelvic-floor-muscles) before piling on volume.
- **Quitting at week two.** Muscle change takes time. Most studies ran for 8 to 16 weeks — judging results after a few days is like weighing yourself after one gym session.

## Common questions

**Can I do too many kegels?** Yes. Beyond about 40 reps a day the muscle fatigues, and chronic over-clenching can lead to a tight, achy pelvic floor. Rest days are fine.

**Do kegels help premature ejaculation?** The evidence is encouraging. Pelvic floor training gives you a stronger, faster contraction to consciously delay climax — see our guide on [how to stop premature ejaculation](/blog/how-to-stop-premature-ejaculation) for how to apply it.

**What if I have ED?** Roughly half of men aged 40 to 70 experience some ED, per the Massachusetts Male Aging Study, and pelvic floor training is a legitimate first-line, drug-free option — the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) lists it among the standard approaches. **How do I know it's working?** Firmer morning erections, better control, and less post-urination dribble are common early signs, usually within 6 to 12 weeks of daily training.

## The bottom line

You don't need a PDF — you need the right muscle, the right reps, and the discipline to do them daily. The routine is simple: locate the pelvic floor, do 4 sets of 10 holds, add fast squeezes, and repeat every day for at least six weeks. The science behind it is real, and the payoff — firmer erections, more control, better bladder function — is worth the few minutes a day.

The only variable left is consistency. Handle that, and the muscle does the rest.
