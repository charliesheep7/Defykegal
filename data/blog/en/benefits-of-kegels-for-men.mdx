Kegels have an image problem with men. The exercise was popularised for women after childbirth, most men have never been taught to do one, and there is no visible muscle to check in the mirror.

Studies have examined erectile function, ejaculatory control, and post-surgical continence, with different designs and treatment programmes. Here is what the research actually reports, with the real numbers.

## The research: different people, different treatments

| Question                               | People studied and treatment                                                                                            | What this can tell you                                                                                                              | What it cannot tell you                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Erectile function                      | Men with erectile dysfunction; pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback and lifestyle advice, compared with advice alone | The [Dorey randomised trial](https://bjgp.org/content/54/508/819) supports considering a supervised programme for selected patients | It did not test the Defy app, penile enlargement or every cause of erectile difficulty                   |
| Ejaculatory control                    | A small group of men with lifelong premature ejaculation receiving pelvic floor rehabilitation                          | The [Pastore study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4003840/) reported improvement during a rehabilitation programme       | An uncontrolled study cannot isolate the effect of home contractions or predict an individual's response |
| Urinary control after prostate surgery | Men recovering from radical prostatectomy receiving pelvic floor re-education                                           | The [Van Kampen trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10675166/) examined continence in a specific surgical setting                | Its findings are not a universal routine for men without that surgery                                    |

These programmes, patient groups and outcomes are not interchangeable. An exercise app can help with a habit; it was not the intervention tested in these studies. Ask a clinician which approach, if any, fits your circumstances.

## Benefit 1 — Firmer, Longer-Lasting Erections

The mechanism is mechanical, and it is the part most men have never had explained.

An erection is a pressure system. Arteries dilate and flood the two spongy chambers of the penis with blood — that is the inflow side, driven by nitric oxide. But holding that pressure requires sealing the veins that drain it. Two pelvic floor muscles do that work: the ischiocavernosus and the bulbospongiosus, which wrap the base of the penis and compress its outflow.

Erectile difficulties have many possible causes. A symptom alone does not establish pelvic floor weakness or veno-occlusive dysfunction. An assessment can help identify appropriate treatment; this article does not compare exercises with medication or advise stopping a prescribed treatment.

That is why the Dorey results matter. They support further consideration of pelvic floor training for selected patients, not the claim that it addresses every cause of erectile dysfunction. ## Benefit 2 — Real Ejaculatory Control

Ejaculation is driven substantially by rhythmic contraction of the bulbospongiosus and the surrounding pelvic floor. The Pastore study explored pelvic floor rehabilitation in a small group of men; it does not establish that strengthening alone gives every reader control over ejaculation.

The study's design and combination of treatments matter. It does not establish that home strengthening is superior to other treatment options or that an app reproduces the programme. Our [premature ejaculation guide](/blog/how-to-stop-premature-ejaculation) discusses the broader context; persistent symptoms should be assessed rather than managed by increasing repetitions alone.

## Benefit 3 — Bladder Control, Prostate Recovery, and Orgasm

The pelvic floor also forms the support structure for the urethral sphincter, which is why the benefits extend well past sex.

- **Urinary control.** [Cleveland Clinic's guidance on kegel exercises for men](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/22211-kegel-exercises-for-men) notes that strengthening these muscles helps prevent or improve urinary and faecal incontinence. The appropriate approach depends on the cause of leakage — see our guide to [urge incontinence](/blog/urge-incontinence) for the different patterns.
- **Post-prostatectomy recovery.** This is the strongest evidence base of the lot. Pelvic floor training measurably speeds the return of continence after prostate surgery, and a [systematic review of exercise and continence after robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10461030/) concluded that pelvic floor muscle training improves continence outcomes. Starting before surgery appears to help.
- **Post-urination dribble.** The bulbospongiosus is what clears the urethra after you finish. Weak, and the last of it arrives in your underwear a minute later. This is one of the fastest benefits to appear.
- **Orgasm intensity.** Less formally studied, but consistently reported: since orgasm involves the same muscles contracting, stronger muscles tend to mean a stronger sensation.

## How long until results?

Research programmes are assessed over weeks or months, but they do not establish a fixed personal timetable. Record the routine you followed, whether you could relax between contractions and any difficulties, then review those notes with the professional advising you.

Do not use a lack of improvement as a reason to keep increasing repetitions. New or worsening pain, difficulty emptying the bladder or difficulty relaxing warrants advice. Symptoms alone cannot tell you whether muscles are weak or overactive.

## Getting Started

1. **Find the muscle.** It is the one you would use to stop urine mid-stream, or to lift your testicles slightly without moving anything else. Use the stop-stream test once to identify it, then never again as an exercise — doing it habitually can irritate the bladder.
2. **Do both contraction types.** Short one-second pulses for the fast-twitch response that matters during sex, and 5 to 10 second holds for endurance. Most men only ever do one.
3. **Choose an appropriate schedule.** A study protocol is not automatically the right routine for an individual.
4. **Progress the load.** Add hold time, add reps, or train standing rather than lying down. Progression should depend on your ability and symptoms, not a fixed calendar.
5. **Check you are the right candidate.** If you have pelvic pain or urinary urgency, your floor may be too tight rather than too weak — our guide on [telling if your pelvic floor is tight or weak](/blog/how-to-tell-if-pelvic-floor-is-tight-or-weak) covers the distinction, and it matters.

## Common Questions

**Can I do kegels sitting at my desk?** Yes, and that is part of the appeal — nobody can tell. Lying down is easiest when learning because gravity is not working against you; standing is hardest and therefore the best progression.

**Do I need a device or weights?** No. Devices exist and some men like the feedback, but every trial cited here used unweighted contractions, some with biofeedback. Consistency matters far more than equipment.

**What if I cannot feel the contraction at all?** That is common and usually means the muscles are genuinely weak or the coordination is poor. It typically resolves within a couple of weeks of practice. If it does not, a men's pelvic floor physiotherapist can assess it directly — see our overview of [pelvic floor muscles](/blog/pelvic-floor-muscles) for the anatomy first.

**Will kegels make my penis bigger?** No. They change firmness and control, not size. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling something.

## The Bottom Line

Pelvic floor training may help selected men, but the studies do not all test the same programme or establish an individual success rate. Technique, diagnosis, supervision, and the treatment setting matter. This page explains the evidence; use the beginner and anatomy guides above for your next questions.
