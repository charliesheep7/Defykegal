Search for average penis length by age and you will find charts with neat per-decade figures — 21 to 30, 31 to 40, and so on. Almost all of them are invented. No large study has ever produced credible adult length averages broken down by decade, because there is nothing there to find.

Here is what the actual data says, why the age question mostly dissolves under scrutiny, and what genuinely does change over a man's life.

## The Best Data We Have

The reference point is a systematic review by [Veale and colleagues, published in *BJU International* in 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25487360/). It pooled 17 studies covering up to **15,521 men**, all measured by health professionals using a standard procedure rather than self-reported. That last detail matters enormously — self-reported figures run consistently and predictably high.

The averages:

- **Flaccid length:** 9.16 cm (3.6 inches)
- **Flaccid stretched length:** 13.24 cm (5.2 inches)
- **Erect length:** 13.12 cm (5.2 inches)
- **Flaccid circumference:** 9.31 cm (3.7 inches)
- **Erect circumference:** 11.66 cm (4.6 inches)

Two things worth pulling out. First, stretched flaccid length and erect length come out nearly identical — which is why clinicians measure the stretched flaccid state as a proxy. Second, the standard deviation for erect length was 1.66 cm, meaning roughly two-thirds of men fall between about **11.5 and 14.8 cm** (4.5 to 5.8 inches). The distribution is much tighter than the internet implies.

The review also found the strongest consistent correlation was between length and **height**, with correlation coefficients ranging from 0.2 to 0.6. That is a weak-to-moderate relationship — real, but nowhere near strong enough to predict anything about an individual.

## Growth by Age: What Actually Happens

The age question has a real answer, but only for one stretch of life.

**Childhood to puberty.** Almost no change. The penis grows roughly in proportion with the rest of the body and stays small until hormones shift.

**Puberty (roughly 9 to 14 at onset).** This is where all the growth happens. Testicular enlargement comes first, then penile growth in length, then in girth, driven by testosterone. The whole sequence typically runs over three to five years.

**Age 16 to 18.** Growth completes for most men. Some see minor changes into the early twenties, but the substantial development is done.

**Adulthood.** Length is stable. There is no natural progressive decline built into ageing.

The variability inside puberty is the part that causes most of the anxiety. Two 15-year-olds can be three years apart in developmental stage while being the same age in years. A boy who starts puberty at 14 and one who started at 11 are not comparable, and comparing them produces panic over a difference that will have disappeared entirely by 20. Our post on [when the penis stops growing](/blog/when-does-your-penis-stop-growing) covers the timeline in detail.

## Why There Is No Adult Age Chart

The nomogram study built single curves across all adult ages rather than separate charts per decade, and no comparably sized dataset has demonstrated a meaningful adult age trend. Any chart showing a smooth per-decade decline is extrapolating from nothing.

That said, three real things change how the penis looks and functions over time — and none of them is an age curve.

### 1. The Suprapubic Fat Pad

The most common cause of apparent shrinkage. Weight gain adds fat to the pad above the base of the penis, burying part of the shaft. The organ has not changed; the visible portion has. It is also the one form of apparent shrinkage that clearly reverses — losing abdominal weight uncovers the buried portion of the shaft.

### 2. Erection Quality

As erections become less rigid, the erect state is simply less full. Blood flow declines with vascular ageing, and the pelvic floor muscles that trap blood in the penis weaken from disuse. The result reads as smaller, but it is a filling problem, not a size problem — and unlike actual length, it responds to training.

### 3. Specific Medical Causes

[Peyronie disease](/blog/peyronie-disease) produces scar tissue in the tunica albuginea, causing curvature and often shortening. Radical prostatectomy is associated with penile shortening in a substantial share of men. Both are identifiable conditions with medical management, not ageing.

## The Question Underneath the Question

Most men searching for length data by age are not doing research. They are checking whether they are normal, or whether something has changed.

On the first: the data above says the range is narrower than you think, and most men who worry are comfortably within it. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/penis) and [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/mens-health/average-penis-size) both put the clinical threshold for a genuine micropenis at a stretched flaccid length under 9.3 cm in adults — a condition affecting a very small fraction of men.

On the second: if something feels different, the likely candidates are the fat pad and erection quality, in that order. Both are addressable.

There is also a persistent gap between what men worry about and what partners report caring about. Research on sexual satisfaction consistently points to erection firmness, stamina, and control as the factors that matter in practice. Those three have something in common: they are all trainable.

## What You Can Actually Change

You cannot lengthen the penis without surgery, and the results of length surgery are frequently disappointing relative to the risk. Pills, pumps used for enlargement, and stretching devices do not produce durable length gains — our breakdown of [penis enlargement treatments](/blog/pennis-enlargement-treatment) goes through what the evidence shows.

What responds:

**Erection firmness.** The ischiocavernosus and bulbocavernosus muscles wrap the base of the penis and compress the veins during an erection, keeping blood trapped inside. Stronger muscles hold pressure better, and a fuller erection is a bigger erection in every dimension that matters during sex.

The clinical evidence is good. In a randomised controlled trial published in the *British Journal of General Practice* in 2004, [Dorey and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15527607/) put men with erectile dysfunction through structured pelvic floor muscle training. After six months, **40 percent had regained normal erectile function** and another **35.5 percent had improved significantly** — with exercise alone.

**Visible length.** Losing abdominal fat uncovers what the fat pad is hiding. This is the single most reliable way to look bigger, and it is not a trick.

**Stamina and control.** Ejaculatory timing is a function of pelvic floor coordination and learned reflex control, and it improves with the same training.

## What to Ignore

**Self-reported survey data.** Men overestimate. Any figure not collected by a clinician with a ruler is worthless for comparison.

**Charts with per-decade adult averages.** Fabricated, or extrapolated from a study that never made that claim.

**Measurements taken differently from the research.** The standard method presses the ruler firmly against the pubic bone along the top of the shaft, which compresses the fat pad. Measuring from the surface of the fat pad instead can cost you two centimetres on paper and explains a lot of unnecessary anxiety.

**Anything sold with a before-and-after photo.** If a supplement could change penile tissue dimensions, it would be a regulated drug with a clinical trial behind it.

## Common Questions

**Does height predict penis size?**
Weakly. The nomogram review found correlations between 0.2 and 0.6 with height — a real statistical relationship, but far too loose to predict any individual. Shoe size, hand size, and nose size have no established relationship at all.

**Does penis size shrink after 40?**
Not the organ itself, in the absence of a specific condition. What changes is the fat pad and erection fullness. Both are worth addressing on their own terms.

**Is stretched flaccid length the same as erect length?**
Close enough that clinicians use it as a proxy. In the Veale data, stretched flaccid averaged 13.24 cm against 13.12 cm erect.

**Do kegels make the penis bigger?**
No, and any source claiming otherwise is selling something. Pelvic floor training improves erection rigidity, so an erection can be fuller and harder — but the tissue itself does not grow. Our guide to [kegel exercises for men](/blog/kegels-for-men) is clear about what it does and does not do.

**Should I be worried about my size?**
If your stretched flaccid length is above roughly 9.3 cm, you are anatomically in the normal range, and persistent distress about size is worth discussing with a doctor as its own issue. Body dysmorphic concerns about the penis are common and treatable.

## The Bottom Line

Average erect length is about 13.1 cm (5.2 inches), based on the largest pooled dataset available. Growth happens during puberty and finishes in the late teens. There is no credible adult age chart, because adult length does not follow one.

What does change with age is how full your erections are and how much of the shaft your body composition hides. Both of those are worth your attention, because unlike length, both actually move when you work on them.
