Search for foods to eat for a strong erection and you get lists of "natural Viagra" — oysters, watermelon, dark chocolate. Some of those foods are genuinely good for you. None of them works like a pill.

What food can do is slower and more useful: keep the blood vessels that feed an erection healthy, flexible and responsive. The evidence for that comes from studies following tens of thousands of men for more than a decade.

Here is what those studies found, the foods that fit the pattern, and where diet stops and training takes over.

## Why food matters for erections at all

An erection is a blood-flow event. Arteries in the penis relax and widen, blood rushes in, and the pelvic floor muscles squeeze the veins at the base to keep it there.

The trigger for that artery relaxation is **nitric oxide**, produced by the inner lining of your blood vessels, called the endothelium. When that lining is healthy, it releases nitric oxide on demand. When it is damaged by high blood pressure, high blood sugar, smoking or chronic inflammation, the response weakens.

The arteries in the penis are narrow, so they often show that damage first. That is why erectile problems can appear years before heart symptoms — and why the same foods that protect your heart tend to protect your erections.

## What the big studies found

Three studies do most of the heavy lifting here.

**The Mediterranean diet study.** Bauer and colleagues followed 21,469 men from the Health Professionals Follow-up Study and published the results in [JAMA Network Open in 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33185675/). Men under 60 with the highest Mediterranean diet scores had a **22% lower risk** of developing erectile dysfunction than men with the lowest scores. The link was also present, though weaker, in men in their 60s and 70s.

**The flavonoid study.** Using the same cohort, Cassidy and colleagues analysed 25,096 men in the [American Journal of Clinical Nutrition in 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26762373/). Higher intake of three flavonoid groups — anthocyanins, flavanones and flavones — was linked to lower ED risk, with reductions of 11-16% in men under 70. Men with the highest total fruit intake had a **14% lower risk**.

**The weight-loss trial.** In a randomised trial in [JAMA in 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15213209/), Esposito and colleagues gave 110 obese men with ED either detailed advice on eating less and moving more, or general healthy-living information. After two years, erectile function scores rose in the intervention group and stayed flat in the control group. About **one in three** men in the intervention group improved.

The first two are observational, so they show association rather than proof. The third is a real trial, but it combined diet with exercise and weight loss. Together, the message is consistent: **eating pattern matters more than any single food.**

## 9 foods worth putting on your plate

These foods fit the patterns the research rewards. Think of them as regulars, not rescue meals.

1. **Berries.** Blueberries, blackberries and strawberries are rich in anthocyanins, one of the flavonoid groups linked with lower ED risk in the Cassidy study.

2. **Citrus fruit.** Oranges and grapefruit are the main dietary source of flavanones, the group that showed the strongest association in the same study.

3. **Red grapes and cherries.** Another anthocyanin source. Note the grapes, not the wine — alcohol in larger amounts works against erections. See [how alcohol affects a man sexually](/blog/how-does-alcohol-affect-a-man-sexually).

4. **Leafy greens and beetroot.** Spinach, arugula and beetroot are high in dietary nitrate, which your body converts to nitric oxide. Arugula and beetroot are higher-nitrate choices than kale, as we covered in [kale for erections](/blog/kale-for-erections).

5. **Watermelon.** A natural source of L-citrulline, which converts to arginine and then nitric oxide. In a small study in Urology, a 1.5 g daily citrulline supplement moved 50% of men with mild ED to normal hardness, versus 8.3% on placebo. Food doses are lower, so keep expectations modest.

6. **Extra virgin olive oil.** The core fat of the Mediterranean pattern, and a straightforward swap for butter and processed oils.

7. **Nuts.** Walnuts, almonds and pistachios supply unsaturated fats and arginine. They are a staple of the Mediterranean diet score.

8. **Oily fish.** Salmon, sardines and mackerel provide omega-3 fats that support blood vessel health and fit the healthy-eating indexes used in the big cohort.

9. **Legumes and whole grains.** Beans, lentils and oats help control blood sugar and cholesterol — two of the biggest drivers of endothelial damage.

## What to cut back on

Adding good food works best when you also remove what damages the system:

- **Processed and red meats** in large amounts
- **Sugary drinks and refined carbohydrates**, which push up blood sugar
- **Heavy drinking**, which blunts arousal and erection quality in the short and long term
- **Ultra-processed snacks** that add calories without much else

High blood pressure is a common hidden driver here, and our article on whether [high blood pressure causes erection problems](/blog/does-high-blood-pressure-cause-erection-problems) explains the mechanism.

## Where diet stops and training starts

Food improves the supply side — how easily blood flows in. It does very little for the other half of an erection: the muscles that **hold** blood in.

That is the pelvic floor's job. The bulbocavernosus and ischiocavernosus muscles compress the veins at the base of the penis to maintain rigidity. In Dorey's 2004 randomised trial in the British Journal of General Practice, 40% of men with ED regained normal erectile function after pelvic floor exercises, and another 35.5% improved. No dietary study reports numbers like that, because training the muscle is a direct intervention.

The best approach combines both: eat for your blood vessels, train for your pelvic floor.

## What progress looks like

Set a realistic timeline. Blood vessel health changes over months, and the trial evidence runs over one to two years.

- **Weeks 1-4:** Better energy and fewer blood sugar crashes. Erections may not change yet.
- **Months 2-3:** If you are also losing weight, exercising and training your pelvic floor, many men notice firmer and more reliable erections.
- **Months 6-24:** The window where the big studies measured meaningful differences.

If erections changed suddenly, if morning erections have disappeared, or if you have chest pain on exertion, see a doctor. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) notes that erection problems can be linked to conditions such as heart disease and diabetes, and those are worth catching early.

## Common questions

**Do oysters help?** Oysters are very high in zinc, and zinc deficiency is linked to low testosterone. If your zinc levels are normal, extra oysters are unlikely to change much.

**Is dark chocolate worth it?** Cocoa contains flavonoids, but the specific groups that showed the clearest link in the Cassidy study came mostly from fruit. A square of dark chocolate is fine; a bar a day is mostly sugar and calories.

**Should I take supplements instead?** Whole foods have the stronger and broader evidence. If you are considering supplements, read our breakdown of [vitamins for ED](/blog/vitamins-for-ed) first.

## The takeaway

The foods to eat for a strong erection are the ones that keep your blood vessels healthy: berries, citrus, leafy greens, nuts, olive oil, oily fish and whole grains, with less processed food and alcohol. Expect change over months, not hours. Pair that eating pattern with regular exercise and pelvic floor training, and you are working on both the flow and the hold.
