Finding a cut on your penis is unsettling, but the most likely explanation is a simple one. Genital skin is thin, and friction during sex or masturbation — especially without enough lubrication — can split it.

Most of these cuts heal quickly with basic care. A smaller number are a sign of something that needs treatment: a skin infection, a tight foreskin, or a sore that only looks like a cut.

Here is how to tell the difference, how to help a cut heal, and when to get it looked at.

## Why the skin on your penis cuts so easily

The skin of the penis is some of the thinnest on your body. It has to stretch a lot during an erection, and in uncircumcised men the inner foreskin and glans are kept moist, which makes them softer and easier to tear.

That same skin has an excellent blood supply. It is the reason minor injuries there can bleed more than you expect, and also why they usually heal fast.

A cut is more likely when the skin is already under strain: when it is dry, inflamed, stretched tightly, or rubbed repeatedly without lubrication.

## Common causes of a cut on the penis

**Friction during sex or masturbation.** The most common cause by far. Vigorous or prolonged activity, a dry partner, or not enough lubricant can cause small splits, often along the shaft or the foreskin.

**A frenulum tear.** The frenulum is the small band of skin on the underside of the penis, where the foreskin meets the head. It can tear during sex, sometimes with a surprising amount of bleeding. Minor tears usually heal on their own; a frenulum that tears repeatedly may be too short.

**Balanitis and yeast infections.** The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/balanitis/) describes balanitis as inflammation of the head of the penis, often caused by a yeast infection, poor hygiene or irritating soaps. Inflamed skin cracks easily, and splits often come with redness, itching or a white discharge under the foreskin.

**A tight foreskin.** When the foreskin cannot easily pull back over the head of the penis — called [phimosis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/phimosis/) — it can split when stretched during an erection or sex.

**Shaving and grooming.** Nicks from razors or trimmers are easy to miss at the time and show up as small, straight cuts afterwards.

**Skin conditions.** Lichen sclerosus causes white, fragile patches that tear easily. Eczema and psoriasis can also affect genital skin.

## What else a "cut" could be

Some sores look like cuts but are not caused by injury. This matters, because the treatment is completely different.

**Genital herpes.** According to the [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/herpes-simplex-virus), an estimated 520 million people aged 15 to 49 worldwide have HSV-2, the main cause of genital herpes. It usually starts with tingling or burning, followed by small blisters that break into painful sores.

**Syphilis.** The first sign of syphilis is usually a single, painless sore. It is easy to mistake for a minor injury. In a 2022 case report in Oxford Medical Case Reports, a man's penile wound after masturbation was initially treated as a frenulum tear; tissue analysis later showed it was a [primary syphilis chancre](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36299666/).

The lesson is not to panic about every nick. It is that a sore you did not clearly injure, one that does not heal, or one that appears after a new partner deserves a test.

## How to care for a cut on the penis

For a minor cut from friction or shaving:

1. **Stop any bleeding** by pressing gently with a clean tissue or cloth for a few minutes.
2. **Rinse with warm water** and pat dry. Skip perfumed soap, alcohol and hydrogen peroxide — they irritate and slow healing.
3. **Protect it** with a thin layer of plain petroleum jelly if it is rubbing on your underwear.
4. **Wear loose, breathable cotton underwear** to reduce friction and moisture.
5. **Pause sex and masturbation** until the skin has fully closed, usually a few days to a week.
6. **Use plenty of lubricant** when you resume. Water-based or silicone-based lubricant both work; check condom compatibility.

Most men see clear improvement within two to three days.

## Recurring splits and the diabetes link

If your skin keeps splitting, look for a cause rather than just treating each cut.

Yeast infections are one of the most common drivers, and blood sugar plays a part. Sugar in urine feeds yeast, which is why genital yeast infections are more common in men with diabetes. A class of diabetes drugs called SGLT2 inhibitors works by pushing sugar out through the urine. In a [2019 study in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30207042/) of more than 150,000 men, those starting SGLT2 inhibitors had about **2.7 times the risk** of genital infections, including balanitis, compared with men starting another diabetes drug.

If you have recurring splits plus frequent urination, thirst or tiredness, ask your doctor about a blood sugar test. And if you take a diabetes medication, mention genital symptoms rather than stopping the drug on your own.

## When to see a doctor

Get it checked by a GP or sexual health clinic if:

- The cut has **not improved within a week** or is getting worse
- You notice **blisters, ulcers, warts or several sores**
- There is **discharge, a bad smell, spreading redness or swelling**
- The skin **keeps splitting** in the same place
- You had **unprotected sex or a new partner** before it appeared
- You have **fever or swollen lymph nodes** in the groin
- You cannot pull your foreskin back, or it gets stuck behind the head

Seek urgent care for heavy bleeding that will not stop with pressure, or a sudden popping sound with pain, swelling and loss of erection during sex — those are signs of a penile fracture, which is a different and serious injury.

## Keeping penis skin healthy long-term

A few habits prevent most friction cuts:

- Use lubricant generously, especially for longer sessions
- Wash with warm water only; avoid scented products
- Dry the area properly, including under the foreskin
- Moisturise dry skin with a fragrance-free emollient
- Use condoms with new partners

## Common questions

**Is bleeding from the frenulum dangerous?** It can look alarming because the area has a small artery, but most frenulum tears stop bleeding with firm pressure for 10 minutes. If it will not stop, go to urgent care.

**Should I use antibiotic cream?** Not routinely. Clean, dry and protected is enough for most minor cuts. If you think it is infected, see a doctor.

**Can a cut affect erections?** A minor skin cut does not damage erectile tissue. Pain can make erections uncomfortable while it heals. If erection problems continue after healing, read our overview of [erectile dysfunction treatment](/blog/erectile-dysfunction-treatment).

## The takeaway

A cut on the penis is usually a friction tear that heals within days with gentle care and a break from sex. What deserves attention is the pattern: cuts that keep coming back, sores you did not clearly injure, and anything that appears after a new partner.
