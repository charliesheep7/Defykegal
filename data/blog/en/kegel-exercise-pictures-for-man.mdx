If you went looking for kegel exercise pictures, you already know the frustration: most diagrams show a cross-section of the pelvis with an arrow, and you're left thinking, *okay, but what am I actually supposed to do?* The truth is that a kegel is an internal movement. No photo can show you the one thing that matters — whether you're squeezing the right muscle.

So instead of another static diagram, this guide paints the picture in words: exactly what a correct kegel looks like from the outside, what it feels like on the inside, and how to run the routine that research actually backs.

## Why kegels work — the muscle behind the picture

Picture a hammock of muscle slung from your tailbone to your pubic bone. That's your pelvic floor. Two of its muscles wrap the base of the penis and, when they contract, help trap blood in the erectile tissue and drive it upward. Train them and you get firmer erections and better control over ejaculation.

This is well-documented. In a randomized controlled trial led by physiotherapist Grace Dorey, published in [*BJU International*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16104916/), men who trained their pelvic floor saw **normal erectile function restored in 40% of cases, with a further 35.5% improving**. The same muscles control your bladder, which is why pelvic floor training is a first-line tool for urinary leakage as well.

The catch is form. These muscles are invisible, so it's easy to *think* you're training them while actually clenching your abs. That's exactly the gap a picture can't close — but clear physical cues can.

## What a correct kegel looks and feels like

Here's the visual you actually need, described from two angles.

**What it looks like from the outside:**

- The base of the penis draws slightly inward.
- The scrotum and testicles lift a touch.
- Your stomach stays flat and still.
- Your buttocks and thighs don't move at all.

If you stood in front of a mirror, a correct kegel would be almost undetectable — just a small lift at the base. That subtlety *is* the sign you're doing it right.

**What it feels like on the inside:**

- A gentle "lift and squeeze" sensation between the anus and the base of the penis.
- The same feeling as stopping yourself from passing gas.
- The same muscle you'd use to stop urinating mid-stream.

## Step-by-step: the routine in motion

Follow these steps in order. This routine lines up with Mayo Clinic's guidance for men and the [step-by-step method from Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/bladder-and-bowel/step-by-step-guide-to-performing-kegel-exercises).

1. **Find the muscle.** Once, while urinating, stop the stream. The muscle that did that is your pelvic floor. Use this only to locate it — not as a regular exercise, since interrupting flow repeatedly can irritate the bladder.
2. **Confirm the picture.** Contract that muscle and check the mirror cues above: base lifts, everything else stays still. Rest a hand at the base of the penis to feel the movement if you're unsure.
3. **Do a slow hold.** Squeeze for 3 to 5 seconds, then fully relax for the same time. The release is half the exercise.
4. **Build a set.** Repeat for 10 slow holds. That's one set.
5. **Do 4 sets a day.** Forty quality reps total. Don't chase more — extra volume fatigues the muscle instead of building it.
6. **Add quick squeezes.** Once slow holds feel easy, add 10 fast contract-and-release reps. These train the split-second reflex behind ejaculatory control.
7. **Progress the hold.** Over the weeks, extend your slow holds toward 10 seconds.

## Building the habit

A routine you can picture perfectly still does nothing if you skip it. Because kegels are invisible, they're the easiest exercise in the world to forget — there's no soreness, no mirror gains, nothing external to remind you.

Anchor them to daily triggers: one set brushing your teeth, one at a red light, one in bed before sleep. Nobody around you can tell, which is exactly why they're easy to slot in anywhere.

## Common mistakes the pictures don't warn you about

- **Clenching everything.** If your abs, glutes, or thighs are tensing, you're compensating and under-training the target muscle. Isolate it.
- **Holding your breath.** Breathe normally through every rep. Bracing and holding air recruits the wrong muscles.
- **Skipping the release.** Relaxing fully between reps is part of the exercise. A muscle that never lets go gets tight, not strong.
- **Overdoing it.** More than 40 reps a day can leave the pelvic floor tight and achy — which performs worse. If you already feel tension, read [how to relax the pelvic floor muscles](/blog/how-to-relax-pelvic-floor-muscles).

## Common questions

**Do I need special equipment?** No. Kegels are a bodyweight movement. The muscle doesn't know or care whether you used a gadget — it responds to correct, consistent contraction.

**How do I know I'm improving if I can't see it?** Track the outcomes instead of the muscle: firmer morning erections, less post-urination dribble, and better control during sex are the real progress markers. Our [kegels for men](/blog/kegels-for-men) guide sets realistic milestones.

**Is it safe to do these every day?** Yes, at 40 reps a day. If persistent ED or leakage is your reason for training, the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) recommends a check-up to rule out other causes first.

## The bottom line

The best "kegel exercise picture" for men isn't a diagram — it's the small, almost invisible lift at the base of the penis while everything else stays still. Nail that cue, run 4 sets of 10 every day, add fast squeezes, and give it six weeks. The research is solid and the payoff — firmer erections, better control, stronger bladder function — is real. The only thing no picture can supply is showing up daily.
