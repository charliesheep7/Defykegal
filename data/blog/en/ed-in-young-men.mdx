Most men think of erectile dysfunction as something that happens at 60, not 25. So when it shows up in your 20s or 30s, it can feel like something is badly wrong with you.

**ED in young men is common, it usually has an identifiable cause, and most of those causes can be treated.** Here is what the research says about why it happens, how to tell what kind you might have, and what you can do about it.

## ED in Young Men Is More Common Than You Think

The idea that ED only affects older men does not hold up.

- **One in four new ED patients was 40 or younger.** In a [2013 study in The Journal of Sexual Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23651423/), Capogrosso and colleagues looked at 439 men seeking help for ED for the first time. 114 of them, or 26 percent, were 40 or younger, with ages ranging from 17 to 40.
- **Almost half of those young men had severe ED.** The rate was 48.8 percent, similar to the 40 percent in older men.
- **Prevalence may be as high as 30 percent.** A [2017 review in Sexual Medicine Reviews](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642047/) by Nguyen, Gabrielson and Hellstrom found large multinational studies estimating ED in young men at up to 30 percent.

If you are dealing with this, you are far from alone. And because it is common, doctors know how to work it up.

## Occasional Problems vs Real ED

Not every off night counts as ED. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) says most men occasionally fail to get or keep an erection, usually because of stress, tiredness or too much alcohol, and that this is nothing to worry about.

It becomes worth checking when it keeps happening. If you want a simple way to gauge where you stand, our guide on [how to check for ED](/blog/how-to-check-for-ed) walks through the signs.

## Mind or Body: The Two Main Types

Doctors usually sort ED into two broad groups. The 2017 review describes how they tend to look different.

### Psychological ED

Causes include depression, anxiety and relationship difficulties. According to the review, men with this type tend to have:

- A **sudden** start to the problem
- **Lower libido**
- **Good** erections during sleep, on waking or when alone

That last point is a useful clue. If your body can produce a solid erection on its own, the plumbing is often working.

### Physical ED

Physical, or organic, causes involve blood vessels, hormones, nerves, medicines or the structure of the penis. Men with this type usually notice a **gradual** decline and a low to normal libido.

**Physical causes are not rare in young men.** A [2015 review in the Asian Journal of Andrology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25370205/) by Papagiannopoulos and colleagues notes that while psychological ED is more common in younger men, at least 15 to 20 percent have a physical cause.

## Common Causes and Risk Factors

The NHS lists several conditions that can cause recurring ED:

- High blood pressure or high cholesterol
- Diabetes
- Depression or anxiety
- Hormone problems
- Side effects of some medicines

Lifestyle stands out in younger men. In the Capogrosso study, **young men with ED were more likely than older men to smoke and to use illicit drugs.** They also had fewer other health problems and higher average testosterone, which suggests their ED often comes from habits or psychology rather than chronic disease.

Alcohol and medicines are worth a closer look too. See our guides on whether [alcohol causes ED](/blog/does-alcohol-cause-ed) and the [drugs that can cause ED](/blog/drugs-that-cause-ed).

## Why You Should Not Ignore It

It is tempting to wait and hope it fixes itself. But ED can be an early signal of a wider health problem.

The 2015 review notes that **physical ED has been shown to predict increased future health problems and mortality**, which is why the authors recommend a thorough workup for any man with sexual problems. The NHS also says recurring erection problems may be a sign of a treatable condition.

For a young man, that is actually good news. Catching high blood pressure, high cholesterol or a hormone issue at 30 gives you decades to do something about it.

## What Helps

### Lifestyle Changes

The NHS recommends a set of changes that can help:

1. **Lose weight** if you are overweight.
2. **Stop smoking.**
3. **Eat a healthy diet.**
4. **Exercise daily.**
5. **Reduce stress and anxiety.**
6. **Keep alcohol to 14 units a week or less.**
7. **Ease off cycling** for a while if you ride more than 3 hours a week.

Given what the Capogrosso study found about smoking and drug use, the first few items are especially relevant for younger men.

### Pelvic Floor Training

Two pelvic floor muscles, the bulbocavernosus and ischiocavernosus, press on the base of the penis during an erection and help keep blood in. Training them is a direct, low-risk way to support erection quality.

In a [randomized trial in BJU International](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16104916/), Dorey and colleagues studied 55 men with ED. Men taught pelvic floor exercises plus lifestyle changes did significantly better than men given lifestyle advice alone. **After 6 months, 40 percent had regained normal erectile function and another 35.5 percent had improved.** For how the whole process works, see our guide to [how an erection works](/blog/how-does-an-erection-work).

### Medical Treatment

The 2017 review notes that PDE-5 inhibitor tablets remain the mainstay of treatment, and the NHS lists sildenafil and tadalafil among the main options. If a physical cause is found, treating it comes first. Our guide to [ED treatment options](/blog/erectile-dysfunction-treatment) compares the full range.

### Talking Therapy

If anxiety, depression or relationship strain are driving the problem, treating them is part of treating your ED. Ask your doctor what support is available, including talking therapies.

## When to See a Doctor

The NHS advises seeing a GP or going to a sexual health clinic if erection problems keep happening. Expect questions about your lifestyle and relationships, a blood pressure check and possibly a genital exam or blood tests.

## The Bottom Line

ED in young men is common, often severe enough to matter, and usually linked to something you can identify. Psychological causes lead in younger men, but physical causes are real for at least one in five. Get recurring problems checked, clean up the habits the research flags, and add pelvic floor training to the plan. It is a low-risk habit that supports whatever else you try.
