In the middle of a meeting. On the bus. First thing in the morning for no obvious reason. If you have ever wondered why do men get boners at the most random times, the answer is that **most erections have nothing to do with conscious arousal.**

Your body has three separate routes to an erection. Only one of them starts with sexual thoughts. Understanding all three explains the awkward ones — and tells you a surprising amount about your sexual health.

## How an erection happens

Every erection follows the same basic process, whatever triggers it.

According to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/penis), the shaft contains spongy chambers — the corpora cavernosa and corpus spongiosum — that fill with blood to create an erection.

Here is the sequence:

1. **Nerves send a signal** to the blood vessels in the penis.
2. **Smooth muscle relaxes** in the arteries and spongy tissue, letting blood rush in.
3. **The chambers swell** and press against the veins that normally drain blood away, trapping it inside.
4. **Pelvic floor muscles at the base of the penis contract**, raising pressure and adding rigidity.

When the signal stops, the smooth muscle tightens again, blood drains out, and the penis softens. For a deeper look, see our explainer on [what an erection is and how it works](/blog/erection-meaning).

## Type 1: Psychogenic erections (the brain)

This is the type most people think of. Sexual thoughts, images, sounds or smells are processed in the brain, which sends signals down the spinal cord to the penis.

Psychogenic erections are also the most easily disrupted. Stress, anxiety, distraction and fatigue all interfere with the brain's signal. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/erection-problems-erectile-dysfunction/) notes that most men occasionally fail to get or keep an erection, usually because of stress, tiredness or alcohol.

## Type 2: Reflexogenic erections (touch)

These come from physical stimulation, and they do not need the brain to start them. Touch or friction on the penis or nearby area sends signals to a reflex centre in the lower spinal cord, which triggers the erection directly.

This is why men get boners from:

- Clothing rubbing against the penis
- Vibration, such as on a bus, train or motorbike
- A full bladder pressing on nearby nerves
- Washing or accidental contact

It is also why men with some spinal cord injuries can still have reflex erections without feeling aroused. Reflexogenic erections often feel random precisely because your conscious mind had nothing to do with them.

## Type 3: Nocturnal erections (sleep)

Men have several erections every night, mostly during REM sleep. Morning wood is simply the one you notice because you woke during or just after it.

Nocturnal erections are not caused by sexual dreams. They appear to be part of the body's normal sleep physiology, and may help keep erectile tissue healthy by regularly flooding it with oxygen-rich blood.

They also change with age. In a sleep laboratory study of 40 healthy men aged 23-73, published in the [Journal of Gerontology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3418036/), the frequency and duration of sleep erections decreased progressively with age. Most men over 60 did not have full sleep erections, even though they reported regular sex.

## Why random boners are more common when you are young

Teens and young men get far more spontaneous erections. Higher hormone levels, a highly responsive nervous system and a reflex pathway that fires easily all play a part.

As men get older, erections tend to need more direct stimulation and take longer to build. That is normal ageing, not a problem in itself. ## What your erections say about your health

Because the three types use different triggers but the same plumbing, they give you useful clues.

**If you get morning or random erections but struggle during sex,** the blood vessels and nerves are probably working fine. The NHS notes that depending on the cause, men with erectile dysfunction may still get erections at other times, such as when they wake up. That pattern often points to stress, anxiety or relationship factors.

**If morning erections have faded along with sex drive,** hormones may be involved. In the European Male Ageing Study of 3,369 men aged 40-79, published in the [New England Journal of Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20554979/), three sexual symptoms — poor morning erections, low sexual desire and erectile dysfunction — were the ones most clearly linked with low testosterone. Our guide to [low testosterone symptoms](/blog/low-testosterone-symptoms) explains what to check.

**If all erections have weakened,** blood flow is often the issue, which can be linked to blood pressure, diabetes or heart health. That is worth a medical check.

## Where the pelvic floor fits in

Whatever triggers an erection, two pelvic floor muscles help maintain it. The ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus wrap around the base of the penis and squeeze to increase pressure inside the erectile chambers.

When these muscles are weak, erections may be less firm or fade sooner. In a randomised trial by Dorey and colleagues in the [British Journal of General Practice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15527607/), pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback helped **40%** of men with erectile dysfunction regain normal function, and another **34.5%** improved.

Cleveland Clinic also lists kegel exercises among the habits that support penis health, alongside regular exercise, sleep, less alcohol and not smoking.

## Dealing with an unwanted boner

Spontaneous erections usually fade within minutes once the trigger passes. To speed things along:

- Shift your attention to something neutral, like a work task or mental maths
- Change position or stand up and walk
- Take slow, deep breaths
- Empty your bladder if it is full

## When to see a doctor

Get medical help if you have:

- An erection lasting **more than 3-4 hours**, especially if painful. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/priapism/) advises going to A&E, as priapism can cause permanent damage.
- Erections that are regularly too weak for sex
- A noticeable loss of morning erections alongside low sex drive or fatigue
- A new bend, lump or pain in the penis

## The bottom line

Why do men get boners? Because the body has three routes to an erection: the brain, touch-based reflexes and sleep. Random and morning erections are normal signs that your nerves and blood vessels are working. Paying attention to changes in each type can flag stress, hormone or blood flow issues early — and a strong pelvic floor helps make the most of every erection you get.
